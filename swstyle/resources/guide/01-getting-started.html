1. Getting started

What you need

  • FreeCAD 1.1.0 or newer. FreeWorks is built and tested on FreeCAD 1.1.4 for Windows and Linux.
  • Nothing else for parts, assemblies and drawings. Sheet metal and weldments are included.
  • For the Slicer tab, a desktop slicer program. The add-on can download one for you (see chapter 7).

Install

During the private alpha the add-on is not in the Addon Manager: install it by hand, as the README in the zip says. The Addon Manager steps below are for the public release.

The short version is below; chapter 10 has the details, offline installs and fixes for common problems.

With the Addon Manager: in FreeCAD choose Tools, Addon Manager, search for FreeWorks, click Install, then restart FreeCAD.

By hand: copy the FreeWorks folder into the Mod folder of your FreeCAD user data folder and restart FreeCAD.

SystemFolder
Windows%APPDATA%\FreeCAD\Mod
Linux~/.local/share/FreeCAD/Mod
macOS~/Library/Application Support/FreeCAD/Mod

First start

The add-on restyles the window each time FreeCAD starts. On a new FreeCAD profile you may first see FreeCAD's own welcome page, which offers a FreeCAD Light and a FreeCAD Dark theme. Pick one; you can change it later in Options.

On the first start the Welcome box opens alone, with Set Up FreeCAD at the top: Theme (FreeCAD Light or Dark, applied to the window and the Welcome at once), Language, Units (MMGS: millimetre, gram, second, to begin with), Dimension standard (ISO to begin with) and Mouse navigation (the first item, Default, is picked). Choose what you use and press Done: the box goes away and the Welcome shrinks back to its normal size. Every one of these can be changed later in Options.

The window opens on the Welcome box (Ctrl+F2 brings it back). Its Home tab has one row to start a Part, Assembly or Drawing (or Advanced... and Open), your Recent Documents across the full width with a View all link, Recent Folders and Resources side by side below it, and a strip of tips along the bottom with arrows to page through them. An empty list says No recent documents or No recent folders. From there, or with New (Ctrl+N), choose Part, Assembly or Drawing. New documents are numbered Part1, Assem1, Draw1 and so on.

While Welcome at start-up is on, FreeCAD's own Start page is kept from opening (it comes back when you turn the Welcome off, and Help, Start Page still opens it). The one exception is the very first start, when FreeCAD's first-start panel lives in the Start page. The New FreeCAD Document box opens at one size for Novice and for Advanced, with a wide, centred FreeCAD Tutorials button; Advanced adds a Preview box beside the template list and a second Tutorials button beside Novice.

Your first hour

This walk-through takes about an hour. It makes a small bracket, rounds its corners, sets it on a plate in an assembly and puts the bracket on a drawing. Install comes first (above); then the steps run in the order you would use them on a real job: part, sketch, dimensions, feature, a second part, the assembly, the drawing. Every command has the name you would expect, so if you have used a feature-based CAD program before, you will know where things are: New Part, Line, Smart Dimension, Extruded Boss/Base, Fillet, Make Drawing from Part.

1. New Part

Press Ctrl+N, choose Part, click OK. The status bar says "Editing Part". The feature tree on the left shows History, Sensors, Annotations, Material <not specified>, Front Plane, Top Plane, Right Plane and Origin, with the blue rollback bar at the bottom. Nothing is drawn yet, because a new part shows no origin and no planes.

2. Sketch on the Front Plane

  1. Click Front Plane in the tree, then Sketch on the pop-up toolbar. The view turns to face the plane and the red origin appears.
  2. Choose Line on the Sketch tab. Click on the origin to start, then move the pointer to the right.
  3. Watch the small yellow square beside the pointer: it shows the relation the next click will add. A pale dotted line shows when you line up with a point you have hovered over. Click about 60 mm along (do not worry about the exact length), then move up and click, then left and click, then down and click on the first point to close the shape.
  4. Each side took a Horizontal or Vertical relation by itself, and the first point is Coincident with the origin. You added none of them by hand. Pick a side to see its relations in the property panel.
  5. The sketch is blue: it can still move. Try dragging a corner. The shape stays square because the relations hold it.

Tips while you draw: F3 turns snapping on and off, Ctrl while clicking gives that click no snap and no relation, and Esc ends the tool (Esc again leaves the sketch).

3. Smart Dimension

  1. Choose Smart Dimension. Click the bottom side, then click where the number should sit.
  2. Type 60 and press Enter. The first dimension scales the whole sketch so it stays in proportion.
  3. Do the same for the left side, and for any side still blue. When nothing can move, the sketch turns black (fully defined). The status bar says so too.
  4. If you add a dimension the sketch already has, it asks whether to make it a reference (driven) dimension. Say yes; it only shows a number.

4. Extruded Boss/Base

Click Extruded Boss/Base on the Features tab. The sketch closes and the extrude panel opens where the tree was, with a see-through yellow preview. Type a depth (for example 10) and press Enter. Enter is OK and Esc is Cancel; both are also in the confirmation corner at the top right of the view.

The tree now shows Boss-Extrude1 with its sketch under it. Double-click it to reopen the same panel with your values, or right-click it for everything else. Ctrl+Z takes the feature back; each feature is one undo step.

5. Fillet

  1. Choose Fillet on the Features tab. The panel offers constant size, variable size, face fillet and full round; keep constant size.
  2. Click the four vertical edges of the block, or pick the whole body to take every edge. A box that takes edges is light blue while it waits for a pick.
  3. Type a radius (for example 5) and press Enter. The preview shows the rounded edges before you accept.
  4. Fillet1 joins the tree. Double-click it later to change the radius; the values you typed are kept.

Try dragging the blue rollback bar up above Fillet1 to see the part as it was, then drop it back at the bottom. Ctrl+B rebuilds what changed.

6. A second part: the plate

  1. Press Ctrl+N, choose Part, click OK. Save the bracket first if it asks (Ctrl+S, name it Bracket), then name this part Plate when you save it.
  2. Click Top Plane in the tree, then Sketch on the pop-up toolbar. Draw a rectangle with Corner Rectangle, and give it two Smart Dimension numbers, 100 and 100. It turns black when it is fully defined.
  3. Choose Extruded Boss/Base, type 5 and press Enter. Save with Ctrl+S.

You now have two documents open, Bracket and Plate, each with one feature.

7. First assembly

  1. Switch to the Bracket document and choose File, Make Assembly from Part. A new assembly opens with the bracket already placed and fixed: it shows (f) in the tree.
  2. Choose Insert Component, pick Plate from the list of open documents and click in the view to put it down. It shows (-): it floats until a mate holds it.
  3. With no command running, pick the bottom face of the bracket and then the top face of the plate (Ctrl-pick works too). A small toolbar appears with the mates that fit, the most likely first. Choose Coincident. If the parts face the wrong way, click Aligned / Anti-aligned (or press Alt).
  4. The plate jumps to the bracket and Coincident1 appears in the Mates folder of the tree. One mate leaves the plate free to slide on that face; drag it to see. A second mate such as Distance pins it where you want it.
  5. Ctrl+Z takes a mate back; each mate is one undo step. Save the assembly with Ctrl+S.

See chapter 5 for every mate, SmartMates (drag a part by a face onto another) and moving parts.

8. Make a drawing

  1. Switch to the Bracket document (the drawing is of a part) and choose File, Make Drawing from Part. (Ctrl+N, Drawing also works; you then pick the part in the Model View panel.)
  2. In the Sheet Format/Size box choose a standard size with a title block and click OK. A new sheet is A size, landscape, third-angle projection (with the ISO standard it is A3, first-angle).
  3. In the Model View panel pick Front, check the scale, and click on the sheet. Move the pointer to the right and click again for the side view, and above for the top view. Click at a corner for an isometric. Press Esc when you are done.
  4. Choose Model Items to bring the dimensions you used in the model into all views. Add Smart Dimension or a Note where you need more.
  5. Drag a view by its frame to move it. Aligned views stay in line.
  6. Save with Ctrl+S. The drawing stays linked to the part: change the extrude depth in the part and the views follow.

Going back to plain FreeCAD

Uninstall FreeWorks in the Addon Manager, or remove its folder, and restart. Your documents open in plain FreeCAD; FreeWorks-only features keep their shape but can't be edited there.

If something looks wrong

  • The window looks half styled: restart FreeCAD once. The add-on applies its look at start-up, not while a sketch is open.
  • A tab or button seems missing: right-click the ribbon and choose Customize. Every tab shows while Customize is open, and you can switch tabs back on.
  • A FreeCAD tool you know is gone: nothing from FreeCAD is removed. FreeCAD-only tools are on their own ribbon tab and in the menus.
