7. 3D printing (Slicer tab)

The 3D Slicer tab, after Add-Ins on the ribbon, sends your part or assembly to a desktop slicer and brings the result back when you change the model. It works with OrcaSlicer, Bambu Studio and PrusaSlicer. They are separate free programs and are not changed.

Setting up a slicer

No slicer comes with the add-on. Each one is an optional download that you choose.

  • Slicer Updates (on the Slicer tab) lists Orca Slicer, Bambu Studio and PrusaSlicer, each with its Installed and Latest version. Click the download or Update button for the one you want; the button shows the size ("Download 2.3.1 (128 MB)") and reads Cancel while it downloads, with "Downloading <file>: 45 of 128 MB" beside it. The free disk space is checked first and, if it is too little, the message says how much is needed and how much is free. A lost connection says how far it got and to press Download to try again, and a cancelled or failed download leaves no partial file; the release zip is removed after it is unpacked. The release comes from the program's official release page, its checksum is checked, and it is kept in FreeCAD's user data folder, apart from any copy installed on your computer. Roll Back returns to the version before, What's New shows the release notes, and Check Now looks for new releases.
  • A copy already on your computer is never started by the 3D Slicer tab, and its version is not probed. The tab uses only the copy downloaded here; with none, it offers the download.
  • Slicer Apps (on the Slicer tab) adds another slicer: drop its zip or installer on the box. For an installer, you are shown who signed it and asked before it runs.
  • The full buttons (Orca Slicer (full), Bambu Studio (full), PrusaSlicer (full)) open the real program inside a tab. If that slicer isn't there yet, you are asked whether to download it.

PrusaSlicer 3.0 is still a preview release, and a switch in its Slicer Updates box chooses between preview and finished releases. A downloaded slicer is unpacked into a short folder named for its release (for example 3.0.0-alpha14), so its first start can read its own preset files on Windows; if an earlier download stopped half way, installing again clears it.

  • Settings, printers and sign-in: a slicer downloaded here keeps its own settings, presets and sign-in in its own folder in FreeCAD's user data folder, and starts there. A copy installed on your computer is never read or changed. Tick Use my installed slicer's settings in that slicer's row in Slicer Updates (it is off by default, and shown only when you have a downloaded copy) to share printers, presets and sign-in with the installed copy; it applies from the next start. The first time the downloaded copy starts, the slicer shows its own setup wizard, as a fresh install does, and you sign in there if you want to.
  • First run in Orca Slicer or Bambu Studio: when the slicer has no printer set up yet (a new copy whose setup wizard never ran) and you have not picked one in FreeCAD, the Printer box starts with Set up your printer: a Vendor list and a Printer list from the slicer's own printer profiles, Use This Printer, and Wizard in <slicer>.... Use This Printer does for one printer what the slicer's wizard does, in the slicer's own files, so the slicer finds the printer and its presets there. Wizard in <slicer>... opens the slicer in the tab with its own wizard, which suits setting up several printers at once. This writes only into the settings folder the add-on uses: the slicer's own copy, or your installed slicer's only when you ticked Use my installed slicer's settings. PrusaSlicer needs no setup: with no printer of yours it lists every printer it has. With Orca Slicer 2.5, whether it is the downloaded copy or your own, the Printer, Filament and Process lists show Orca's printers and presets, including vendors whose profiles Orca packs into one file; a printer you add here brings that vendor's filaments and processes with it, and Slice no longer stops with "Preset type could not be resolved".

See Optional downloads for everything that is downloaded on request.

Sending a part

  1. Open the part or assembly.
  2. Click Send Part on the Slicer tab. The part goes to the slicer as one 3MF with the object names, colours and plates (Send Part and Save Project both write that kind of 3MF).
  3. The slicer opens in its own tab inside FreeCAD with the part on the plate. Slice and print there as usual.

Assemblies: "Keep assembly positions" (on by default) sends the parts as one object that keeps their places. Switch it off to send each body as its own object.

After a change: if you change the model, a "Part changed" box asks whether to reload it in the slicer. Yes rewrites the same file and refreshes it there; the part keeps its orientation, position and print settings. Reload Part on the Slicer tab does the same at any time.

Print3D (File, Print3D)

Print3D prepares a part before the slicer. Nothing in the document changes, and the analysis colours are put back when you close it. The panel has two tabs, Settings (printer, scale, orientation, split, export and save) and Preview.

  • Printer: the bed of the printer each slicer has picked in its 3D Slicer tab, one of your own printer profiles (below), or Custom size (typing X, Y or Z makes it Custom). It says whether the part fits (red when it does not). A profile sets the bed, the minimum thickness, the overhang angle, the resolution and the slicer that Send uses.
  • Scale: Scale to fit, or a scale you type (typing turns Scale to fit off).
  • Orientation: pick a flat face for the Bottom face and it lies on the bed. Suggest lists the four orientations that need the least support (then the most on the bed, then the lowest); click one to use it. As Modelled puts it back.
  • Split to Fit: pieces along X, Y or Z worked out from the bed (typing a count turns the split on); the bed box and the cut planes show in the view, and the pieces are what Save and Send use.
  • Preview tab: Show the printer's build plate draws a translucent plate with a 10 mm grid and the build volume. The analysis is Show overhang areas that need support (a threshold angle; red faces, with their area in mm2) and Show areas too thin to print (a minimum thickness; yellow faces). Opening the Preview tab turns both highlights on. Imported meshes get the numbers without colours.
  • Export Options: Resolution Coarse, Fine or Custom (deviation and angle, the same as the STL options in Save As), the triangle count, and Send solids to the slicer (on: the slicer meshes the solids itself; off: it takes these triangles).
  • Save: Send to Slicer (Orca, Bambu or Prusa; the pieces go on the 3D Slicer tab's plate) or Save to File... (a 3MF with one named object per piece, or an STL). The ? button explains it. A part or assembly made of several bodies or components goes as one object for each, with its name and its colour kept, and Send to Slicer puts those colours on the slicer's filament slots. If the slicer is not downloaded, Send says why in red and offers the download from Slicer Updates on the 3D Slicer tab, and Print3D stays open; the Send list shows such slicers as "(not downloaded)". Nothing is printed or signed in to.

Printer profiles: Tools, Options, Export, Print3D Printers keeps a profile for each printer: name, bed X, Y and Z, nozzle, minimum wall, overhang angle, resolution and slicer. New adds one, From Slicer copies the printers each slicer has picked, and Delete removes one. The profiles appear in the Print3D printer list.

The slicer view

The plate is dark with a fine grain and a light grid. The cube at the bottom left turns with the view; click a face of it to look from that side. The box at the bottom right shows the picked object's name, size, volume and triangle count. Settings pages are tabs across the top, grouped under headings, with a lock at the left of each setting.

On the plate

These follow the slicer you have downloaded, and each is in the real program too.

  • Object list: right-click an object for Object Settings or a Height Range Modifier. The values go to the slicer with the part. Brim type is one of the object's settings, with Painted among its choices. Under the list, a table shows the selected object's own settings, and those of each modifier part and height range, by category with the slicer's setting names. Double-click a value to change it (empty takes it off), click the cross on a row to take that setting off, and click the + on a group to open its Add Settings. Any change marks the slice stale. The table shows only when the selected object has something of its own.
  • Build plates: each build plate has a column of icons beside it in the 3D view: Remove plate (only while there is more than one plate; it asks first when the plate has objects), Auto orient and Arrange for that plate, Lock or Unlock, and Edit plate name (the name shows beside the plate at once). A locked plate is left alone by Arrange all and Auto orient all and shows no Arrange or Orient icon. The lock is saved in the project's 3MF file and read back when you open it. Dragging an object moves it, and a click on a plate icon runs it.
  • Painting: Support, Seam, Color and Fuzzy skin painting are on the plate toolbar (keys L, P and N for the first three). Each has Paint and Erase, a Tool type (Circle, a round brush with a brush size that paints the faces turned towards you; Sphere, a ball that paints everything inside it on both sides; Fill; or Smart fill, which paints a face and the smooth surface round it up to an Angle, 30 degrees by default) and Remove all. Support painting also has Gap fill (fills the small unpainted faces enclosed by paint, with a Gap area), Highlight overhang areas (tints the overhangs on the plate) and On overhangs only (the brush, Fill and Smart fill paint only where the object overhangs; the angle starts from the process's support threshold angle). Section view in every painting tool has a slider that cuts the objects away from your side so inner faces can be painted; clicks go past the cut, and all the way right shows the whole objects again. Painted faces are drawn in the tool's colour (fuzzy skin in purple). The paint goes to the slicer inside the 3MF file. The slicer prints painted fuzzy skin only when its Fuzzy Skin setting is not Disabled; the panel says so.
  • Brim ears (Ctrl+E): click an object to put a round ear of brim on the bed under that point; right-click an ear to remove it. Head diameter is 5 to 20 mm. Auto-generate puts an ear at each sharp corner of the first layer (Max angle and Detection radius). An ear that touches nothing is red and counted as invalid. If the object's brim type is not Painted, the panel says the ears will not print and offers Set the brim type to Painted.
  • Split to objects and Split to parts: Split to parts keeps an object made of several solids as one object that moves as one, with one part per solid. The list shows "[N parts]"; right-click it to open Parts, give a part its own filament, or Merge parts. A part with its own filament shows in that colour on the plate.
  • Measure (key U): pick points, edges or faces for the distance with X, Y and Z, an angle, or a circle's diameter.
  • Mesh boolean (key B): Union, Difference or Intersection of two objects (pick them with Ctrl+click, or use the parts of an object split to parts). Object and Tool, Delete input, then the action button. The result keeps the object's name, filament and settings. Paint on the object is dropped because its faces change; an empty result says so and changes nothing.
  • Text (key T): Part (raised text, added to the object as parts of its own so it can print in another filament), Negative part (engraved) or Modifier (the text is a modifier part, shown grey; right-click the object, Parts, the text, Settings... to set what it changes, such as infill density, and optionally its own filament). Choose a font, Height, Depth and Rotation; click a face to put the text on it, then Add text.
  • Assembly view: a view only. Explosion ratio (1 to 3) pulls the objects and their parts apart, each in its filament's colour; Section view (0 to 1) cuts the view at a height to look inside. Closing the tool shows the plate as it was.
  • Variable layer height also colours the object by layer height while the tool is open (blue for thin layers through cyan and yellow to red for thick), with a key under the curve.
  • Calibration, Tolerance test: puts a plate with six hexagon holes (the hole is 10 mm across the flats plus 0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.15, 0.2 and 0.3 mm) and a hexagon key on the plate, after asking, because the plate is cleared. Print both and try the key in each hole to find the clearance your printer needs.
  • Prime tower: when a plate prints with more than one filament and the prime tower is on, it shows as a see-through box on the plate. Drag it; the slice puts it there.
  • Flushing volumes: a dialog sets the purge for each filament change, with a multiplier and Auto-calculate.
  • Variable layer height: Adaptive, Smooth and Reset, with a curve you can edit, and Add plate for a second build plate. Paint, layer heights and layer codes are kept per plate, and Slice all slices every plate from one 3MF file.
  • Preview: the legend adds Line Width, Flow, Layer Time and Filament views, with options for travel moves, retractions and seams, and the filament used. The layer slider has two handles and the moves slider follows the tool head. A G-code window beside it shows the lines around the move. Right-click the layer slider to change filament, or to add a colour change, a pause or custom G-code at that layer; marks show on the slider. The plate toolbar is hidden in Preview.

Watching and controlling a printer

Before a printer is connected the Device panel shows only the printer row with a line on what comes next; the camera, state, temperatures, AMS and controls appear once it is connected, and with no printer at all only + (Add Printer) shows. Light is left out for Klipper and OctoPrint, which name no light. Once connected, the panel shows the printer's state. Its Controls set print speed and fans, move the print head and send it home. Each control asks before it acts. In the settings boxes Reset All shows only when something has changed; Add Filament at the largest number of slots and the Slicer Updates buttons (Download or Update, Release notes, Roll back) when there is nothing to do are hidden instead of greyed.

Slicing without leaving FreeCAD

The Orca Slicer, Bambu Studio and PrusaSlicer buttons on the Slicer tab open a panel with that slicer's printer, filament and process presets, read from the slicer's own settings. The list of settings in the All ... Settings window is built from your own downloaded copy too: for Orca Slicer and Bambu Studio from the presets that come with it (every setting its presets set, with its kind and choices worked out from their values, so a choice list can also be typed into), for PrusaSlicer from the program itself. The pages, labels, units and short descriptions are this add-on's own, and every setting of the Orca Slicer and Bambu Studio lists has a description of its own. The settings most people change show with Advanced off and the rest appear with Advanced on. The list is kept for next time and made again when that copy or its presets change; a setting that none of the presets ever sets is not listed and keeps the slicer's default.

PrusaSlicer uses the downloaded copy from Slicer Updates. Its printer, print and filament presets and its Print, Filament and Printer settings pages come from PrusaSlicer itself; bed size, height and extruder count come from the printer preset. The list of PrusaSlicer's settings is read from your own PrusaSlicer the first time it is needed (its own schema and help text; the pages and labels are the add-on's) and kept for the next time, so the settings pages always match the version you downloaded. Until a PrusaSlicer is there the panel shows no All ... Settings buttons and no empty tabs. Slice runs PrusaSlicer's command line once for each kept plate, with the plate positions kept, and Preview and Export G-code work as below. Paint (support, seam, colour, fuzzy skin), brim ears, variable layer height, modifier parts, the account, open and save project, calibration and the layer-code menu are not shown for PrusaSlicer, because they work only through Orca and Bambu project files. The panel has one filament slot for each extruder of the printer, a picked object says "Picked objects use filament N", the 3MF is written in PrusaSlicer's own project form, and every printer PrusaSlicer has set up is listed. On a printer with more than one extruder or tool head, each part keeps the extruder you picked for it. The full slicer tab keeps looking for the slicer window for longer than 120 seconds. In all three slicer panels the rows share one label column, the settings buttons span the group and the small buttons match the height of the fields.

  • No control in the panel is greyed. Slice, Preview, Export G-code and Send to Printer are always available: with no slice yet they slice first and then do their job; with no slicer program they offer the download (Slicer Updates); with nothing to slice they say what is missing. Send to Printer with no printer shows Add Printer... and lists the new printer afterwards. The layer slider shows only while the preview shows, Reset only when a setting was changed, and Delete plate only when there is more than one plate. Every button on the 3D Slicer tab has its own drawn icon.
  • Cancel: while a slice runs the Slice button is Cancel. It stops the slicer's own run and the meshing at the next body; the panel says "Slicing was cancelled." and the button is Slice again. Export G-code and Preview stay live while slicing and follow the running slice.
  • Progress: the panel says "Meshing <part>, body k of N..." for each body, and shows the time elapsed while the slicer runs (there is no time limit; Cancel stops it).
  • Imported meshes (an imported STL, OBJ, 3MF or PLY) are printable: they sit on the plate as their own triangles and are sliced as they are. Send Part and Print take them too.
  • Slice slices the part with that slicer's engine and shows its slice info: Prepare time, Model printing time and Print time, then one row for each filament used (its colour, metres, grams and cost from the slicer's own G-code), the Total and the Layers.
  • Preview shows the toolpaths layer by layer in the 3D view, with the slicer's legend (each line type with its time and share; click one to hide it, or switch to another view such as speed) and a layer slider at the side.
  • Quitting FreeCAD with the panel open stops the slice, closes the plate tool and ends any printer connection first.
  • Export G-code saves the last slice as G-code, or as a sliced project for Bambu Lab printers.
  • Send to Printer, Add Printer and Log In are for printers on your network or account. Add Printer offers Prusa (PrusaLink) for the MK4, MK4S, XL, MINI+ and CORE One, with the user name and the printer's PrusaLink password (or an API key); the Device panel shows state, upload, Pause, Resume and Stop, and leaves out the camera, light, temperatures, fans, moves and G-code, which PrusaLink cannot do. The bed leveling and timelapse choices in Send to Printer show only for Bambu Lab and Elegoo printers.

Signing in

Bambu Studio's cloud printing needs your Bambu account. Sign-in happens with your email and a code that Bambu sends you, inside Bambu's own program. Your password is never seen here.
