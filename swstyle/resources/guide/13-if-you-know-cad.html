13. If you already know another CAD program

You already know the ideas: sketch, relate, dimension, feature, rebuild. This page tells you where each thing is, which keys and mouse moves you can expect, what is different and why, and how units and decimals behave. Chapter 14 walks you through four whole jobs.

Mouse: wheel zooms about the pointer; middle drag turns the view; middle click a face, edge or point and drag to turn about it; Ctrl + middle pans; Shift + middle zooms; Alt + middle rolls; double-click a feature to edit it; right-click for the menu.

Mouse gestures: hold the right button and flick; four directions is the default, and the ring depends on whether you are in a part, an assembly, a drawing or a sketch. You can choose 2, 3, 4, 8 or 12 directions and change any command in Customize, Mouse Gestures. Chapter 9 lists every ring.

3. What is different, and why

  • It is FreeCAD underneath. Your documents are FreeCAD files, and the geometry engine is FreeCAD's. Share with others through STEP, IGES, STL, 3MF, DXF or PDF (Save As, Options... sets what each one writes).
  • No greyed-out buttons. A control that cannot apply is hidden until it can, and a box that belongs to an option switches that option on when you use it. If you look for a button that is greyed in your other CAD program, look for it to appear once the thing it needs is picked.
  • Some add-in tools are missing. The Simulation, Flow, CAM, Render and similar tabs run on the FreeCAD workbench behind them (CalculiX, Elmer, the CAM and Render workbenches). Where a tool has nothing behind it, it is left out rather than shown dead. Motion Analysis is basic: good for how a mechanism moves, not for engineering loads.
  • Feature names and results can differ in small ways. Sketches, features and mates are named the usual way (Boss-Extrude1, Cut-Extrude1, Fillet1, Mate names), but rebuild results come from FreeCAD's kernel. When an edit seems to give a different face count or a failed rebuild, the property panel says why instead of staying silent.
  • Extra FreeCAD tools are kept. A tab with FreeCAD-only tools, and a FreeCAD submenu at the bottom of the tree menu, give you FreeCAD's own commands.
  • The Design Library has no online content folders. The vendor's online content and 3D component roots are left out, because they cannot work offline. Your own library folders, the Toolbox and the forming tools folders are there.
  • Simulation meshing has limits. Standard and curvature-based meshes, Mesh Control and the mesh quality plots are available. Not offered: the blended curvature-based mesh, an element size growth ratio, a mesh size for each part, and remeshing only the failed parts. A part with slivers or self-intersections can fail to mesh ("Meshing failed: Gmsh made no mesh of <part>."). Very large or very fine meshes are slow. A very thin sheet meshed as a solid can solve to an unrealistically large deflection without a warning, so check the displacement scale. Chapter 11 has the messages.
  • Display mode is kept per component. In an assembly, the Display Pane's Display Mode applies to the one component you picked, is saved in the assembly and does not change the part's own file.
  • Plain FreeCAD is one step away. The add-on restyles FreeCAD each time it starts. Chapter 1 explains how to turn it off without losing your documents.

4. Units and decimals

  • One units box. Click the units box at the right of the status bar to switch the document between the metre, centimetre, millimetre and inch systems. Custom... is ticked when the document uses another system (feet and inches, for example). Tools, Options, Document Properties, Units sets the unit and the decimals for Length, Dual Dimension Length, Angle (2 decimals at first), Mass/Section Properties and Mass. Picking another length unit there switches to Custom.
  • The part's own unit everywhere. Reference geometry, 3D curves, panel labels and result boxes show the part's unit, and the decimals you set apply at once on every panel.
  • Typing a number. In an inch part a plain number in an equation, a global variable, Dimension View or a panel box is read as inches. A dimension or a length global variable can be added to a plain number ("D1@Sketch1" + 5) and the result keeps the unit. You can always type the unit with the number (12 mm, 0.5 in) to override it.
  • What you read. The status bar shows the pointer position and measurements with the unit glued on, the Length decimals you set and no trailing zeros ("37.7mm", "12mm"), and never "-0mm". Angles follow the Angle decimals.

5. Where your habits meet something different

Configurations, tables, hardware, rebuild, undo and files, one at a time.

Configurations. The tabs above the tree switch it to the configurations, the display states and the property panel. Add Configuration in a part has Advanced Options with "Suppress features" and an "Add Rebuild/Save Mark" box. A derived configuration stays in its parent's branch: rename the parent and the branch follows; delete it and the branch goes with it. A depth, angle, Dome size or Fillet radius set per configuration changes only in the configuration you are showing when you type a new value in the feature's panel; the others keep theirs, and Cancel leaves all of them as they were. Custom properties have a Configuration specific switch, and File Properties, Properties Summary shows each configuration's values. When you insert a part with configurations into an assembly, a list lets you choose one. Different: a Bill of Materials row for a configuration shows the bare part name.

Design tables. A design table drives configurations from a spreadsheet. A $PARENT column names the configuration a row derives from. With New configurations switched off in the design table's options, Add Configuration still makes the configuration and only the table leaves it out; typing it into the table brings it back. Deleting a design table stops it applying its Edit Control and Options.

Toolbox. Tools, Toolbox holds standard hardware (bolts, screws, nuts, pins and more) by ISO, DIN, ANSI Metric and ANSI Inch standard. Configure Component, Smart Fasteners and the Design Library drops list the lengths the standard gives for the size you choose; changing the size refills the list and keeps the nearest length. Toolbox Settings chooses which sizes are offered. Different: metric sizes run M3 to M24 (button head screws stop at M16, countersunk head screws at M20); the length ranges are an approximation of the standards' tables, not a copy; ANSI Inch has no retaining rings yet; DIN hex bolts and nuts use DIN's own across-flats and nut heights, not ISO's.

Hole Wizard. Type tab for the kind, standard and size, Positions tab for where; counterbore, countersink, plain, tapped and slot holes. In a part with two or more bodies the Type tab also shows Feature Scope. Different: a size you leave out in Toolbox Settings, Hole Wizard is left out of the Hole Wizard's counterbore, countersink, straight tap and screw clearance and tap drill lists too (drill, dowel and pipe sizes stay); a DIN hex counterbore uses DIN's across-flats; ANSI Inch normal fit clearance for #4 to 1/2 follows the ASME B18.2.8 table.

Rebuild and undo. Ctrl+B rebuilds what changed; Ctrl+Q rebuilds everything. The rollback bar and the Freeze bar work as you expect, and with Enable Freeze bar on, the rollback bar stops at it. Each feature is one undo step, so one Ctrl+Z takes back a whole feature; inside a property panel Ctrl+Z takes back the last pick or value. A sketch started on a plane is one undo step. Different: undo is FreeCAD's, whose Undo list is 20 steps by default. The Equations box and the Fillet and Draft panels keep their own short undo for their own steps. When a rebuild fails the property panel says why, and a step that fails quietly writes one line in the Report view.

File formats. Your documents are FreeCAD files (.FCStd). To exchange: Save As to STEP (AP203, AP214 or AP242), IGES, STL or 3MF, and from a drawing PDF, DXF or SVG; Save As, Options... sets what each writes (STEP can keep or drop face and edge colours; STL has deviation and angle boxes). VRML, OBJ, glTF and pictures have no options. Pack and Go copies a document with the files it refers to, with a nested or a flat file list. Different: opening another program's own files directly is limited; STEP is the dependable route. Features that only this add-on makes keep their shape in plain FreeCAD but cannot be edited there.
