6. Drawings

Drawings show parts and assemblies on paper-sized sheets with views, dimensions, notes and tables. They are built on FreeCAD's TechDraw, so drawings stay linked to the model.

Starting a drawing

  1. Ctrl+N, Drawing. The Sheet Format/Size box opens.
  2. Choose a standard size (with or without a title block), or a custom size. A new sheet is A size, landscape, third-angle projection; with the ISO standard it is A3, first-angle.
  3. The Model View panel starts by itself, and the View Palette opens beside it. Pick an open part or assembly, or Browse.
  4. Choose the view (Front, Top, Isometric and so on), the display style and the scale, then click on the sheet. Move the pointer and click again for projected views; a click at a corner gives an isometric. Press Esc when done.

The drawing takes the model's name once the first view is placed.

From a part you can also use File, Make Drawing from Part.

Views

Relative, Predefined, EmptyViews set up by faces, ahead of time, or blank

A section view hatches its cut faces with the ANSI patterns (ANSI31 to ANSI38, read from the ANSI.pat file in your FreeCAD folder; stock FreeCAD has none, so the add-on supplies it). A detail view of a section view hatches the cut faces inside its circle like the section. Section View's panel shows Partial section and Hide cutting line shoulders only once the cut makes them apply, and its Display Style buttons are live (a click turns Use parent style off). Seen end-on, a cosmetic thread draws a thin three-quarter circle in ISO, DIN, JIS, BSI, GOST and GB drawings (the open quarter at the upper right); ANSI keeps the full circle. View, Display has a fourth row, Tangent Edges With Hidden Ends, which shows the visible tangent edges with their ends hidden (Visible, With Font and Removed turn that off).

The drawing look: no orange fill when the pointer is over a view or a face is picked, a click inside a view picks the view (faces can still be picked with Filter Faces on, or by starting Drawing Tools, Centerline Faces, Area Annotation (Faces) or Area Annotation with nothing picked and then clicking the face), and the view name is not drawn under a view on hover or pick. Standard 3 View keeps its three views inside the border and off the title block, stepping the scale down on an empty sheet until they fit; with Options > Drawings > Automatically scale new drawing views off, the first view keeps the sheet scale. Esc closes the picked view's panel and drops the pick in one step, and an Area Hatch scales with its view and stays over its area. With a picked view or dimension's panel open, the arrow keys, Delete and Ctrl+Shift+Z keep working on the sheet and Esc drops the pick. Enter repeats the last command on a sheet. Area Hatch/Fill clicked inside a closed sketch entity of a drawing hatches that sketch profile, and a part's Sketch Picture shows in every drawing view that looks square at it when Display sketch pictures in drawings is on.

A drawing is renamed after its model once the first view is placed, and its FeatureManager tree shows the drawing's own rows, not the part's. Click a view to open its panel, titled with the view's name: orientation, display style, scale, and which edges show. A projected or auxiliary view's panel has an Arrow check box in its group title; it adds or removes the arrow and its view label. Its group order is Reference Configuration, Arrow, then Options. Drag a view by its frame to move it; aligned views stay in line. Right-click for Lock View Position, alignment and tangent-edge options.

Dimensions and annotations

  • Model Items brings in the dimensions you used in the model, for the whole model or a picked feature, into all views. Source starts on Entire model and remembers your last choice; with Selected feature or component and nothing picked, OK keeps the panel open and says what to pick. Entire model also brings in a revolve's sketch dimension from its axis, each fillet's radius, a shell's thickness and a circular sweep's profile diameter. Dimensions that land on top of each other on one side of a view are stacked, shortest nearest, an Offset between dimensions apart.
  • Smart Dimension works as in sketches: pick, then place.
  • Ordinate, Baseline and Chain Dimension: for Ordinate, click the zero (an edge, a vertex or a hole), click where the dimensions go (above or below the view for horizontal, left or right for vertical), then click each edge or hole: every click adds its dimension at once, and a hole measures to its centre. Baseline and Chain also add on every click. Esc keeps what you have placed.
  • Model Items: Destination view(s) shows only for Entire model or Only assembly, and clicking a view while Import items into all views is on turns that off and lists the view. Entire model brings in one dimension for each part dimension, not one for every repeat in each view. Double-click a feature dimension (an extrude depth, a pattern spacing) to open the part's Modify box; the part rebuilds and the drawing follows.
  • Ordinate, baseline, chain, chamfer and angular running dimensions; Autodimension; align and auto-arrange tools.
  • Center marks stay on their holes after you save and reopen the drawing, and Rotate View turns a view with its sections, details and marks once each. A diameter or radius dimension can be shown as a linear dimension (Display As Linear).
  • Dimension panel: the precision list runs None to .12345678 with the document's setting as the last item; the tolerance list ends with "Same as nominal (Document)" and "Same as nominal". Geometric Tolerance: Auto Leader is pressed; Straight, Bent and Perpendicular Leader; Set Horizontal and Set Vertical; the Leader/Frame Style lists run from Solid to Chain thick and from 0.18 mm to 2 mm, then Custom Size. Surface Finish and Geometric Tolerance hide Straight/Bent and the arrow while the leader is None. Datum Feature: Square and Round (GB) buttons follow the document style; four leader-end buttons. Note: the Text Format buttons are in rows; the insert is called Insert Surface Finish Symbol. Centre marks, centerlines and cosmetic threads are drawn in the thin line (0.18 mm), not the outline weight.
  • Note, Balloon and Auto Balloon (adds only the missing ones). A click on the sheet places the note and opens it for typing, so the keys go into its text; a note you never typed in is dropped on OK. Auto Balloon's panel has six Balloon Layout buttons, Balloon text (Item Number, Quantity, Custom Property or File Name, with a box for the property name), a Size list that includes User Defined with its own size box, and Lower text for balloons split by a line. A box shows only when the setting uses it. The Balloon panel has a Flag Note Bank group (folded) that places a flag note from the sheet's bank. A balloon's Custom Property or File Name on an assembly view comes from the ballooned component, not from the assembly.
  • Dimension zeroes: dimensions follow Document Properties, Dimensions, Zeroes, Trailing zeroes: Smart (120 but 12.50), Show (120.00) or Remove (12.5). A tolerance under 0.1 mm is written in millimetres, not micrometres. In Edit Sheet Format the status bar says "Editing Sheet Format1". A drawing's Hidden Lines Visible shows its hidden lines as the view is made, a big shaded view is not squeezed, and Shaded With Edges draws the shading under the edges.
  • Drawing panels and saving: the nine drawing panels fit the 300 pixel pane, and Document Properties pages scroll (Tools, Options fits a 768 pixel screen). Ctrl+S no longer asks to rebuild every time, and the drawing is not marked as changed after it saves. Ctrl+Z with the Dimension panel open leaves no stray Drag Dimension step. Opening the Drawing View panel no longer makes the sheet jump. Tables use the document font like dimensions, and the Weld Table columns are a little wider.
  • Dimension panel: a box shows only when its setting applies (the Dual Dimension rows until Dual is on, tolerance precision once a tolerance is chosen, the Break Lines gap once Break Lines is on); a box that is shown sets its option when you use it (typing an Override value turns Override on). Placing a Hole Callout opens this panel on it, with the Value tab (Style, Tolerance, Primary Value, Dimension Text, Dual Dimension) and the Leaders tab. Auto Balloon and Balloon have a More Properties button once a balloon is placed: it opens the Note panel (Text Format with Angle, Font height and Use document font, and Layer) for the last balloon.
  • Note, Surface Finish and Geometric Tolerance have one Leader control, the row of leader buttons (Leader, No Leader and so on). A Center Mark on a hole that already has one redraws that mark, never a second.
  • Geometric Tolerance panel: the symbols are in this order: Straightness, Flatness, Circularity, Cylindricity, Profile of a line, Profile of a surface, Parallelism, Perpendicularity, Angularity, Circular runout, Total runout, Position, Concentricity, Symmetry. The leader group is Leader, Multi-jog Leader, No Leader (the default), Auto Leader, then Straight or Bent and the arrow. A Multi-jog leader is placed by clicking the tip, then each jog, then the same point twice. Picking Straight or Bent with No Leader turns Leader on. Leader Style and Frame Style groups have Use document display (on at first), a line style and a thickness; a pick turns Use document display off and the frame or leader is drawn dashed or thick. Angle comes after them.
  • Surface Finish panel: the symbols are buttons (Basic, Machining Required, Machining Prohibited), Local and All Around are a pair of buttons in the Symbol group, and it has the same Leader group, with Multi-jog.
  • Note panel: the Text Format row has Insert Datum Feature, which inserts |A| with the sheet's next datum label (without using it up); text between bars (|cell|cell|, such as inserted tolerances and datums) is drawn as boxed frames.
  • Precision lists: Unit precision lists ".12 (Document)" first and uses it for dimensions on the document defaults; Tolerance precision lists "(Document)" and "Same as nominal". Both go to 8 places. A dimension on the (Document) item follows Document Properties, Primary precision when it changes.
  • Baseline and Chain Dimension add only the newest dimension on each click, and in a baseline set each new dimension joins the parallel group and takes a new slot, so stacked dimensions are never placed over each other. The Sheet, Bill of Materials, cut list, Hole Table, Area Hatch, Chamfer, Blocks, Move and Configurations panels show no greyed controls, and a sheet tab's right-click menu offers Paste and Delete only when they apply.
  • Weld Symbol opens a floating Properties box with one tab (ISO/ANSI Weld Symbol); OK or Cancel ends the command. Its "Weld symbol..." buttons open the ISO/ANSI Weld Symbols picker, the leader anchor can be None, and Include this symbol in weld table is a box. More views Trimetric and Dimetric stay hidden. A manual Balloon click on a part and then on the sheet places a balloon. Auto Balloon's layout buttons are Top, Bottom, Left, Right, Square and Circular, with Leader attachment Edges; the Balloon panel's style list starts None, Circular, and its Balloon text offers Text, Item Number, Quantity, Custom Properties, Part Number (BOM) and File Name. Area Hatch picks edge by edge.
  • Surface Finish, Geometric Tolerance, Datum, Weld Symbol, Center Mark, Centerline and Hole Callout.
  • A dimension whose edge has gone turns olive. Drag it onto a new edge to re-attach it.

Hole callouts can be placed on bosses, through holes and counts such as 4X, and they follow the hole when its size changes. In a drawing of an assembly, hole callouts, the Hole Table (one row for each hole, SIZE column) and Model Items' Hole Wizard Profile read the holes made in the assembly by Hole Wizard, Simple Hole and Hole Series: for example "2X ⌀6.60 THRU ALL / ⌴ ⌀13.00 ↧ 4.00" or "⌀5.00 ↧ 8.00 / M6x1 - 6H ↧ 6.00". A series is called out by its first part and its tapped last part. A diameter dimension takes the document's default style. Dragging a dimension snaps to the offset distances. Property panels (Hole Callout, Detail View, sheet relations) show no greyed boxes: a box appears when its setting needs it. A three-view layout follows the first-angle or third-angle setting.

Tables

Bill of Materials (top level, parts only, or indented; on a view of a part the panel offers Parts only and leaves out Top-level only, Indented and Flattened), Hole Table, Revision Table, Weldment Cut List, Weld Table and General Table. Double-click a cell to edit it; right-click for formatting: the Formatting rows (Column Width, Lock Column Width, Row Height, Lock Row Height, Entire Table) have no "...", and Table Header is on the table toolbar over the picked cells (Header Top, Header Bottom), not in the cell menu. The Weldment Cut List's Description comes from the item. Attach to anchor point is off at first; OK with no click puts the table on the anchor (attached). Indented numbering is No numbering, Detailed or Flat, and the group Keep Missing Item/Row is there. The Hole Table panel has the groups Tag Type and Scheme. A Bend Table uses a view picked first. The Revision Table has a merged, bold, centred REVISIONS title row, and table editing counts it (cell picks, row heights, Header Bottom). A Weld Table leaves out symbols whose Include in weld table is off. The panel for editing a table has a Stationary corner with four buttons: the table grows away from the corner you pick. A Bill of Materials follows edits to a part's properties (Description, Part Number and BOM quantity). The Hole Table writes X LOC, Y LOC and SIZE at the document's dimension precision ("15.00", "⌀6.00 THRU" at two decimals) unless Tables, Hole precision sets one, and tags holes A1, B1, B2 (a letter for each size, a number for each hole); Combine same tags is off by default and merges the rows when ticked. A Bill of Materials that keeps missing items finds a removed configuration by its part and configuration, so rows show the bare part name. The Bend Table has one row for each bend, with UP or DOWN, and a Closed Corner stays closed when the size changes. A flat-pattern view's bend notes and Bend Table write the inner radius at the document's unit precision ("R 1.00"). The BOM sits above the title block and the revision table at the top right by default.

Sheets

The standard ISO sheet formats (A0 to A4 landscape and A4 portrait) are the add-on's own drawings, and the drawing's tree lists the views and sheets without dimension and note rows.

Sheet tabs along the bottom: click + to add a sheet. Right-click a sheet for Properties (size, scale, projection; the format box shows the file name, with the full path in its tooltip, and Only show standard formats lists the formats of the drafting standard) and Edit Sheet Format (the title block). While the format is being edited it shows blue and the views are greyed.

Saving and output

Save as PDF writes the text of symbols (weld, surface finish, datum) as real searchable text, not outlines.

A new section view is labelled SECTION A-A, with the scale added only when it differs from its parent's, even when you never set Document Properties; the label shows on screen, in the PDF and in a print. A DXF export carries the view labels and, for section views, hatching on the cut faces (not in the oldest DXF format). A drawing saved as a template goes back to the format's own title block text when you start a new drawing from it, instead of keeping the old part's title and material. The Line Font defaults are 0.25 mm for Visible Edges and 0.18 mm for Hidden Edges. A drawing whose model file is missing says so in a box that names the file, and the view is marked "Model not found".

Save as a FreeCAD file, or export PDF, DXF or SVG. Drawings remember the model's state; an out-of-date view is hatched until you rebuild (Ctrl+B).
