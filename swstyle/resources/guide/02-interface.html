2. A tour of the window

From top to bottom: the quick access row, the ribbon, the 3D view with the feature tree on its left and the task pane on its right, the model tabs, and the status bar.

Quick access row

Along the very top: Home, New, Open, Save, Print, Undo, Redo, Select, Rebuild, File Properties and Options. The small arrow beside the logo shows the menu bar (File, Edit, View, Insert, Tools, Window, Help). Move the pointer away and the menus hide again; the pin keeps them shown.

Every menu has access letters. Press Alt and the menu's letter (Alt+F for File), then an item's letter. Opened from the keyboard, the menus underline their letters; opened with the mouse, the letters still work but aren't drawn. When two items share a letter, pressing it again moves to the next one. The letters are set menu by menu (Fillet/Round is F in one menu and another letter in the surface menu), the Window menu has them too (New Window N, Cascade C, Tile Horizontally H), and a row ends in "..." when it opens a box. Every menu row has a picture; a row with no stock picture shows a drawn one.

On the right of the row is the command search box: type part of a command's name and press Enter to run it. A result can also be dragged onto a ribbon tab to add it there as a button.

Ribbon

The ribbon has one tab per kind of work. In a part: Features, Sketch, Sketch Ink, Surfaces, Sheet Metal, Structure System, Weldments, Evaluate, Add-Ins, then tabs for add-ins and a tab with FreeCAD-only tools. Assemblies and drawings have their own tab sets.

  • Turn the mouse wheel over the tab row or the buttons to move one tab left or right.
  • Buttons with a small arrow open a list of related commands (for example the fillet and chamfer family).
  • When the window is narrow the ribbon first drops button text, then shows a » button for whatever doesn't fit.
  • Right-click the ribbon for Customize: choose tabs, button size, text under buttons, and tooltips.
  • Rest the pointer on a button for its tooltip card: the command's name, a picture and a description. The name stays whole at the top of the card, and a button always shows one picture, drawn where the add-on has a drawing for it; with the tooltip style set to without images, the card is text only.

Add-in tabs

Tabs for add-ins appear after Add-Ins, and each one shows only when the FreeCAD workbench behind it is installed; otherwise it stays out of the way instead of showing buttons that do nothing.

ScanRuler files: the ScanRuler tab opens files from its Results list, from Open latest download and from the save prompt of its page (which lists IGES, XYZ, CSV and project files as well). XYZ or ASC point clouds (x y z, with optional normals, which are not kept) become a point cloud, and a flat table of up to 2000 points becomes sketch points. A 2D Measure CSV becomes a sketch at true scale (points, lines, circles and arcs; inches become mm; a spline keeps its start point; dimension and count rows are not read; a sheet in pixels says it is not true size). A .scanruler project opens the scan at true size in the pose it was aligned to, with the reference part, in one group; its Measurements group holds the saved elements as reference geometry (points, lines, planes, circles, cylinder axes), the distances as linear dimensions, and angles and diameters as text notes with the value worked out from the saved geometry; the 2D sheet's saved fits become one sketch. An element with no saved geometry is skipped and counted in the status line. Values of tolerances and geometric tolerances are not rebuilt (a note says so). Each file is one Undo step, and a bad file adds nothing and says why. Lengths in a .scanruler measurement note are written in the document's units (an inch part reads inches; millimetres keep three decimals). On the ScanRuler tab every action button has a tooltip; right-click a result for Open, Open as a Sketch (DXF or SVG), Show in Folder and Copy Path, and right-click elsewhere for Refresh.

  • Simulation (a step-by-step walkthrough is in chapter 11) runs studies on CalculiX: Static, Frequency, Buckling, Thermal and the other types in the list below. Simulation Advisor and Study Properties set one up. Compare Results lists every study side by side (type, whether it ran, largest stress and displacement, lowest frequency or buckling factor) and Show makes one study active. Run All Studies and Duplicate Study are on the Run and New Study arrows. They need a study to act on. The Simulation tab's Connections Advisor makes Contact Sets (a Contact type with an optional Friction coefficient, or Bonded; faces for Set 1 and Set 2, one contact per touching pair, named Contact Set-1 and so on; faces that do not touch give a message and nothing is made).
  • Study tabs and study tree: a part's Simulation studies get tabs along the bottom after Model, 3D Views and Motion Study. Click one to make it the active study; the tree area splits with the study tree below it (the study, the part with its material, Connections, Fixtures, External Loads, Mesh and Results; a thermal study has Thermal Loads instead of Fixtures and External Loads). Click Model or a Motion Study tab and the study tree folds away. Right-click a study tab for Copy Study, Rename, Delete and Create New Simulation Study. Rename in place with F2, double-click a fixture, load or contact set to Edit Definition, and double-click a plot to show it. FreeCAD's own analysis rows no longer crowd the design tree. The Simulation, Flow Simulation, MBD and Motion buttons have their own drawn pictures.
  • Simulation Advisor and study types: the command is named Simulation Advisor. A step box at the top of its task pane page lists 1 Study, 2 Bodies and Material, 3 Interactions, 4 Mesh and Run and 5 Results: the current step is bold, a finished one gets a tick, and each step is a link to its page. With no study yet a Welcome page (Next, and Do not show me this again) leads through five concerns (deformation or stresses, load and unload cycles, sudden collapse, shaking, temperatures) as green-arrow links to a "Create a ... Study" link that makes the study; Back goes back one page. Bodies and Material (with Apply Material) is a page for Static, Thermal, Drop Test, Topology, Nonlinear and the other static-like types. The tools on each page are green-arrow links, and Results Advisor with no results says "There are no results to show yet: run the study first.". Response Spectrum with CQC keeps the sign of each mode's participation factor, so two close modes no longer cancel. The Advisor opens in the task pane with one page per step, and the pages follow the active study's type: for Static, Nonlinear, Buckling, Frequency and Check it is Study, Fixtures, External Loads, Connections, Mesh and Run, Results; Fatigue and Drop Test get their own steps instead of pointing at fixtures a drop test cannot use. The tab's buttons read Fixtures Advisor, External Loads Advisor, Connections Advisor and Results Advisor, and the tab shows Fixtures and External Loads only for the study types that use them. New Study, Design Insight, Topology Study makes a static study with Goals and Constraints (best stiffness to weight, reduce mass by a percentage, 5 to 95) and Manufacturing Controls in its tree. Drop Test Setup takes gravity from a plane, flat face or straight edge (Reverse direction flips it) and has a Target box: Normal to gravity or Parallel to plane, Rigid or Flexible target (flexible asks for a normal stiffness per unit area).
  • More study types: the New Study panel runs top to bottom as General Simulation (Static, Frequency), Design Insight (Topology Study, Design Study), Advanced Simulation (Thermal, Buckling, Fatigue, Nonlinear, Linear Dynamic) and Specialized Simulation (Drop Test, Pressure Vessel Design). Topology Study has three goals (Best Stiffness to Weight ratio, Minimize Maximum Displacement, Minimize Mass with a displacement limit given as a value or a factor of the first maximum displacement) and Manufacturing Controls: Thickness Control, Demold Direction (a face or plane, Reverse, Both directions) and Symmetry (one to three planes); each one set shows as a row in the tree. Pressure Vessel Design combines your static studies (each with a factor) by Linear combination or SRSS, needs them to share one mesh (and says so when a study takes the first one's mesh), runs again first any static study changed since its last run (the progress line says so), and Stress Linearization (right-click a Stress plot) takes two points across a wall and tabulates Membrane, Bending, Membrane + Bending, Peak and Total in N/m^2, MPa, psi or kgf/cm^2. Linear Dynamic has Harmonic (frequency range, points, bias) and Modal Time History (end time, time increment, a load curve), a modal damping ratio (2 percent by default) and plots the step with the largest response. Random Vibration and Response Spectrum Analysis are offered too, with a Uniform Base Excitation load (a base the part sits on that shakes along a direction). Random Vibration takes an acceleration PSD curve (frequency in Hz, g^2/Hz, drawn log-log); Response Spectrum takes a spectrum curve (Hz, g) and a Mode Combination of SRSS, CQC or Absolute Sum; both report RMS results. The base excitation has its own drawn icon. Topology Study with fixtures that hold a non-zero displacement gets the right sensitivity, Minimize Maximum Displacement gives steadier results from run to run, and an input with no energy output stops with a plain message. Flow Simulation tab: Computational Domain over Project, Wizard (asks for the fluid), New (default air project) and Clone Project; General Settings with Internal analysis, Time-dependent (total time and time step shown while ticked), Heat transfer and Project fluid (Air or Water). CAM, Define Machine: the post processor, arguments and output file are kept with the part and taken by its jobs in one Undo step.
  • Flow Simulation (with Elmer): Clone Project, Batch Run, Screen Capture and Engineering Database. A missing Elmer is named in plain words ("Elmer is not installed", with where to set its path) instead of doing nothing.
  • On the Simulation tab, Fixtures and External Loads open panels for the active study (Fixed Geometry, Roller/Slider, Fixed Hinge, forces and so on, each named Fixed-1, Roller/Slider-1 and so on), Create Mesh opens a Mesh panel (a Coarse to Fine slider with Reset, standard or curvature-based mesh, draft quality), and after a run Results lists the study's plots (Stress1, Displacement1, Strain1) with Show, Edit Definition, Chart Options, Probe, New Plot, Delete and Hide Plot. A plot shows on the model with its colour bar, title block and Max mark.
  • Costing and Sustainability take the part's material (a part with none uses Plain Carbon Steel, as a new part does) and follow a material you give the part later.
  • MBD: Size, Location and Angle Dimension take Maximum variation, Minimum variation, Unit precision and Dimension text, show only the rows the tolerance type uses and add the ISO 286 fits (Fit, Fit with tolerance, Fit (tolerance only), with Hole fit and Shaft fit); Capture 3D View can store the configuration and the display state to capture. A tolerance never rounds to zero: 0.1 mm in an inch part reads "0.24 +-0.004".
  • MBD Dimensions: Show Tolerance Status shows every hole of a hole pattern green when the pattern is sized and located.
  • CAM has Probe Operation and CAM Options (opens Options at the CAM pages) beside the CAM workbench's own tools. Post Process, Check Job and Step Thru Toolpath work with nothing picked: they use the part's only job, or the one you picked. Define Machine opens a small Machine window with a Post Processor tab: the post processor, its arguments and the output file. The choice is kept with the part and applies to every job in it and to jobs you make later. One Undo puts the jobs back as they were.
  • ScanTo3D works on meshes; Curvature shows the surface curvature of a mesh. CircuitWorks opens IDF files whatever the case of their file name.
  • Render Tools renders through the Render workbench (Cycles, POV-Ray, LuxCore, Appleseed) when it is installed.
  • Inspection, on drawings, balloons the characteristics and makes the report.

Some tools of those programs have no FreeCAD behind them and are left out.

Feature tree

The left pane lists the document in the order it was built: History, Sensors, Annotations, Solid Bodies, Material, the three main planes, the Origin, then your sketches and features (Boss-Extrude1, Cut-Extrude1, Fillet1 and so on). The Origin row has no arrow: nothing is listed under it, and the axes stay pickable in the view while the origin is shown. With no document open, the area behind the Welcome takes the theme's window colour.

  • Each row keeps its own coloured icon. A feature that needs a rebuild, has an error or is being edited adds a small mark to that icon; the icon itself does not change or turn grey.
  • A sketch shows (-) when it still has free movement and (+) when it has conflicting relations.
  • Rollback bar: the blue bar under the last feature. Drag it up to see the part as it was at that point; features below it turn grey. Right-click it for Roll Forward, Roll to Previous and Roll to End. The last feature's tree icon has no extra mark: the bar alone shows the end. When every feature in a folded folder is rolled back, the folder is grey and the bar sits right above it; a rollback to between two features of a folded folder opens it so the bar sits between them. While the bar is up, the features below it are not rebuilt (they stay as they were; no "Invalid edge link" flash in the Report view) until you move the bar down or choose Roll to End.
  • Solid Bodies rows keep their names after you rename a feature and roll back: a body made by Boss-Extrude1 that you renamed Base stays Base.
  • Folders: pick features, right-click, Add to New Folder.
  • Filter: type in the box at the top of the tree to show only matching rows.
  • Sensors: a mass-properties sensor shows its value and takes its limits in the part's own units (grams in a millimetre-gram-second part), with the unit beside the limit boxes and in the alert; volume, area, density, centre of mass and bounding box sizes work the same way, and sensors made before keep working. A renamed dimension is listed as Width@Sketch1 in the Sensor's dimension list and in Equations.
  • Evaluate units: Thickness Analysis, Geometry Analysis, Interference Detection, Check and Body Compare show and take lengths, areas and volumes in the document's units (inches, square inches and cubic inches in an inch part), not millimetres; calculation itself stays in millimetres. Section Properties follows Options > IPS and reports square inches, inches and inches^4.
  • Equations: one Enter finishes a cell in Add global variable and Add equation, even when the list of names is open (Down and Enter on the list still pick a name). The Equations box has its own Undo and Redo (shown only while they can act) that count only the box's steps, even when FreeCAD's 20-step Undo list is full; Cancel drops every edit made in the box, and a change made outside the (modeless) box is never taken back by the box's Undo.
  • Configurations and design tables: renaming a configuration keeps what hangs on its name: its derived configurations stay in its branch, follow it and are deleted with it, and its Rebuild/Save and Display Data marks stay. Add Configuration in a part has Advanced Options with "Suppress features" and an "Add Rebuild/Save Mark" box. A design table can have a $PARENT column: a row that names its parent is a derived configuration and starts with the parent's values; Edit Table shows the column and the parameter list offers it.
  • Equations that cannot be solved are refused: an equation that refers back to itself (also through a global variable, or between sketch dimensions), a global variable with a syntax error and a dimension equation with a syntax error are each refused with a message that names the dimension, and the earlier equation or value is kept. The same holds in the Equations box, the sketch Modify box (type = and the equation), and the dimension and feature panels. A sketch dimension named like a unit (A, Wb and so on) cannot be used in an equation: rename it.
  • Equations and units: every number in an equation is read in the part's units. A dimension or a length global variable can be added to a plain number ("D1@Sketch1" + 5, "Wall" + 1) and the result goes on the dimension in that unit; in an inch part a plain number in an equation is inches, in the Equations box, in a global variable, in Dimension View, the Modify box and the panel boxes, and Measure... inserts the part's unit. An equation between two dimensions of one sketch ("D2@Sketch1" = "D1@Sketch1" / 3) can be solved. The functions sec, cosec, cotan, arcsec, arccosec, arccotan and sgn read back by their names, 2in reads back as 2in, a global variable's Evaluates to is in the part's unit, and Add equation starts from the dimension's full value, not its shown decimals. Equations made earlier keep working; a new or retyped one takes the new form.
  • Design tables and units: a design table's dimensions are in the part's units: in an inch part a table shows 1 and a typed 2 is 2 in (2in or 50mm typed as written count as written); the Auto-create Dimensions box and the right-click list follow. A dimension that an equation drives keeps its equation, which wins over the table; the table's values for it are not used and the window says so, and Auto-create leaves it out. The right-click list on row 2 offers the part's global variables ($VALUE@name@equations).
  • Configuration Publisher: the parameters are listed by their headers (D1@Sketch1, $STATE@Cut-Extrude1), a feature's state is a Suppressed check box, a number runs from the smallest to the largest value, values are in the part's units and Apply matches numbers, not text. The assembly's Publisher panel shows the same controls.
  • Design tables: with New configurations off in the design table options, Add Configuration still makes the configuration and only the table leaves it out (typing it into the table brings it back). A deleted design table no longer applies its Edit Control or Options.
  • The » at the top right opens the display columns: hide and show, display mode, appearance and transparency per item.
  • Suppressed features: a feature suppressed in the active configuration shows its row in grey text with a greyed icon, not struck out, in Light and Dark; the row is drawn as usual in a configuration where it is not suppressed. The Add Configuration panel is wider so its long Bill of Materials label wraps, and its Color... button follows the swatch (black text on a light swatch, white on a dark one).
  • Renaming, deleting and copying a configuration in the tree: click a configuration again slowly, or press F2, and its bare name can be typed over (not "Long [ Part ]"). Enter renames it as one Undo step; Esc leaves it as it was. The Delete key deletes the configuration you picked, not the one that is shown (a derived configuration asks first, as before). Ctrl+C and Ctrl+V copy and paste it as "Copy of ..."; pasting keeps the configuration you were looking at shown. These keys work in the tree only. A renamed configuration keeps its display states ("Long_Display State 1" becomes "Tall_Display State 1"), and a deleted configuration takes its own display states with it.
  • The tabs above the tree switch to the configurations, display states and the property panel.
  • Display States in the configuration tab: under the configurations a second list shows the display states ("Display States (linked)" while they are linked to configurations). The active state is bold; double-click activates one, F2 renames it, and right-click offers Activate, Add, Rename, Delete and Properties (only what can act; the last or active state offers no Delete). Properties has a Link Display States to configurations box: linked, each configuration lists its own states and Show Configuration returns to the last state used in it.

Property panels

Opening a feature or tool puts its property panel where the tree was. Every panel works the same way:

  • The first box that needs a pick is active (light blue). A box that still needs something is pink.
  • After a box gets its one pick, the next empty box becomes active. Click an item again to take it out.
  • You can pick first: pick a sketch, a face or an edge, then start the feature, and the right box is already filled.
  • Type values with units and simple sums, such as 10in or 2*5mm+1. Type = to link a global variable.
  • The mouse wheel over a value steps it; hold Ctrl for ten times the step.
  • The ? at the top of a panel (or F1, or Help in the panel's right-click menu) opens that panel's entry in this guide. F1 with the pointer on a ribbon button opens that command's entry. Every ribbon command has one, in chapters 17 to 19; the boxes of the File menu and a few others are in chapter 16, and the Help button in each opens its section.
  • Enter = OK (the green check), Esc = Cancel (the red X). Both also sit in the confirmation corner at the top right of the view.
  • The preview is always on: yellow and see-through, with arrows you can drag.
  • The panel remembers your last values for next time. Edit Feature reopens the same panel filled in.

A panel never shows a control that is greyed out: while a panel is open, a control that cannot be used (and its label) is hidden and appears as soon as it can be used. A caption goes with its hidden field (for example Up to: and Offset: on an assembly Extruded Cut, Distance 2: on Chamfer, Pitch: on a weld). A command button that needs something first is not greyed either: it opens its panel and asks for it (a sketch tool with no sketch open asks you to pick a plane or face, then opens the sketch and starts the tool). OK stays available and says in red what is missing. The header Undo and Redo on Fillet, FilletXpert, Draft and DraftXpert appear only while there is a step to take back or put back. File Properties has the tabs Summary, Custom, Configuration Properties and Properties Summary; there is no Apply button (OK keeps the changes). In Options, Messages, Dismissed Messages, Select All shows only while there are messages to pick, and Backup/Recover shows the real auto-recover folder with a Browse... button.

In the 3D view

  • Heads-up toolbar (top middle): Zoom to Fit, Zoom to Area, Previous View, Section View, View Orientation, Display Style, Hide/Show Items, Edit Appearance, Apply Scene, View Settings.
  • Breadcrumbs (top left): the path to what is under the pointer, such as part, body, feature and face. Click a step to pick it.
  • Sheet Metal engine: the add-on carries the sheet metal engine it needs. If none can be loaded at all, each Sheet Metal button offers to install the free SheetMetal add-on with FreeCAD's Addon Manager; the download is optional, and nothing is installed unless you say yes.
  • Quick access buttons: a button that needs a document stays hidden until one is open.
  • Select Midpoint on a model edge makes a real midpoint pick.
  • Pop-up toolbar: after you pick a face, edge, plane or sketch entity, a see-through toolbar appears just above and to the right of the pointer with the commands that fit. It fades if you move away.
  • Hover: with nothing picked, moving over a face lights its edges orange and shows the feature's name.
  • Space opens the orientation box and the view cube round the model. Click a face of the cube to look at it.
  • View moves: the standard views (Ctrl+1 to 8, the orientation box, the heads-up View Orientation), Normal To and Zoom to Fit (F) turn and zoom together in about half a second, easing in and out. Turning with the mouse or zooming with the wheel during a move takes over at once. Pressing Normal To a second time (turning to the other side), Zoom to Selection and Previous View (Ctrl+Shift+Z) ease the same way. A switch between perspective and orthographic still jumps, and if FreeCAD's animation option is off every move jumps as before.
  • Orientation box: the Saved Views row is always shown; Reset Standard Views appears only after a standard view was changed.
  • Perspective: the lens comes from the observer position in View, Modify, Perspective (the default is two object sizes, about 28 degrees; stock FreeCAD uses 45). The camera steps back so the model keeps its size and place on screen, and turning Perspective off gives back the same framing.
  • RealView and scenes: a polished metal (chrome, polished steel) reflects the studio almost like a mirror; plastics, paints and rough metals look as before. The studio is always locked to the model, so it turns with the part and a reflection stays put on it as you turn the view (there is no box for it in Edit Scene). Chrome shows sharp bright streaks where the studio's tall light panels are reflected, with darker gaps between them and a dark horizon band; a face looking straight at you keeps its look, and plastics, matte metals and RealView off are unchanged. The studio the faces reflect comes from the scene you applied, or from 3 Point Faded when none is applied, not from the view background, so chrome stays silver under the Dark theme; a dark scene you pick (Black, Reflective Floor Black, Studio Dark) still gives dark reflections. The Reflective Floor scenes reflect the model, each with its own Floor reflections choice. The Apply Scene list shows your favourites first, then the scene in use if it is not a favourite (ticked), then Theme Background and Manage Favorites; every scene is still in the task pane's Scenes folders. Factory is called Factory Background, and four more scenes were added at the end of the list. Edit Scene has a Basic and an Advanced tab. Basic holds the scene's settings, with a message while Floor shadows is off and a Floor offset. Advanced > Floor Size/Rotation has Fixed aspect ratio, Autosize floor (on at first; the floor is three times the model across), Width, Depth, Aspect ratio and Rotation; typing a Width or Depth turns Autosize off, and with a fixed aspect ratio the other follows. A floor of its own size cuts the floor shadow and the reflection at its edges. Advanced > Environment Rotation turns the studio that RealView Graphics reflects, 0 to 360 degrees, with a slider or a box. Closing the panel without OK puts everything back.
  • Section View: the cap shows the part's own colour, also while the panel is open (Keep cap color gives it the section colour); Graphics-only section starts off; Planar sits above Zonal; Show cosmetic threads shows only when the model has cosmetic threads; Section by Body has a check box in its header, and its list and the Exclude/Include choices work only when it is ticked (picking a body ticks it). In an assembly the last group is Section by Component instead: click a face in the view and its component goes in the box, and Exclude selected and Include selected work for each component. Each component is shown in its own colour (not the whole assembly in one), including its cap, and the group has a header check box that is ticked when you pick a component. After it comes Transparently Section Components (assemblies only; a part does not show it), with its own header check box: a box for components or bodies, Exclude selected and Include selected (Include is the default) and a Transparency of 0.9 with a slider. Click a component in the view, or into the box, to add or remove it; the group is ticked for you, and right-click, Clear Selections empties the box. The cut-away part of each transparently sectioned component stays on screen, see-through at the Transparency and in its own colour (its cap faces are left out). The group is saved with a named section view (Save...).
  • Display Mode of one component: in an assembly the Display Pane's Display Mode applies to the one component you picked, not to every copy of its part. It is saved in the assembly (the part's own file is not touched or marked as changed), it is kept in display states and comes back when you reopen the assembly. Default puts the component back to drawing as its part.
  • Appearances panel: its title is the appearance's name ("color" for a plain colour, or the illumination preset's name). The Selected Geometry filter buttons are pictures (part, face, feature, body; the name is in the tooltip). Basic shows the Color and Image tabs; Advanced shows the four tabs. The colour box has Pick a Color (an eyedropper), the colour, the palette and swatches, a colour spectrum (click or drag to take a colour), R/G/B sliders and RGB / HSV option buttons. Transparency is under Advanced, Illumination, as Transparent amount after Blurry reflection. In Edit Scene the Background group comes first, then Environment, then Floor.
  • Appearances on a face or feature: the Image, Mapping and Surface Finish you choose with Face or Feature picked stay on that face or feature (before, they always went on the whole body). The face's own colour shows with them. Remove Appearance on a face or feature removes its image too, and Cancel puts everything back.
  • Display Pane headings: all four columns have a heading: Hide/Show, Display Mode, Appearance and Transparency.
  • Display Pane button: a > at the right end of the manager tabs opens the Display Pane (< while it is open); it shows only while the feature tree is shown.
  • Shadows In Shaded Mode: the floor shadow has two layers: a tight, dark contact shadow under whatever sits near the floor, and a wide soft shade from the whole outline that shows past the edge in an Isometric view. It is darkest where the model stands on the floor and lighter under parts held high above it. It is drawn again only when the model changes. View Fit frames the model, not its shadow, and Section View does not cut the shadow. Only solid shapes cast one.
  • Four-view layout: Link Views pans and zooms all four views together. Update Standard Views asks before it changes the views. The Camera panel offers Perspective, a standard lens and Roll.
  • Ambient Occlusion: the View Settings option darkens holes, hollows and inside corners, as a real shadow of the surroundings would. It is worked out in small slices so FreeCAD stays responsive, is drawn again when the model changes, and is left out in Wireframe and Hidden Lines.
  • Large models and reflections: the floor reflection and the shadows check the model only when something changed (a part moved, was hidden or rebuilt, or a light was switched), so a big model turns faster with them on. Ambient Occlusion is calculated much faster on a big model, and when you close a document with reflections on, FreeCAD no longer crashes.
  • Level of detail (Options, Performance): in an assembly, while you turn or pan with the middle button or zoom with the wheel, components that are small on screen are left out and come back when the view stops. Off leaves nothing out; More (slower) leaves out specks under 8 px; Less (faster) anything under 40 px. Each left-out component's box is drawn in thin grey. A left-button drag (box pick, component drag) and a plain click change nothing, and nothing in the document changes.
  • Lights, cameras and decals: Add Directional, Point or Spot Light, Add Sunlight and Edit Light open a panel in the task area: On, colour and brightness, and where the light sits (longitude and latitude, or X, Y, Z, with a target and cone angle for a spot, azimuth and elevation for the sun). The Camera panel can show numeric controls (off at first), lock the camera, and take its position or target from a vertex, edge or face; in Camera View the camera follows the view unless it is locked. The Decal panel shows the mask file, colour mask, projection direction and illumination only when they apply. Every light's panel (FreeCAD's own and the ones you add) has Ambient, Brightness and Specularity, each a slider with a percentage box, live; Cancel puts them back. The defaults (Ambient 0 %, Specularity 100 % of Brightness) are the lighting you had before, so nothing changes until you move one. Ambient lifts the dark side; Specularity sets the highlight, and RealView's highlight follows it. In the Display Manager, the Lights, Cameras and scene rows and the appearance rows carry pictures of what they are (a bulb for each kind of light, a camera, a small lit ball in the appearance's colour), and the light and camera panels show the same picture in their header.
  • Planes, decals and scenes: a picked plane draws as a thin light-blue outline with round handles at its corners and edge middles and its name along the top edge, with no fill. A decal is drawn only on its face (cut to the face's outline and holes, and made again when the face or the decal changes). A face with its own appearance shows only that colour even when the body has an image or finish. Scenes, Reflections on floor is a fading mirror of the shaded faces. The Edit Appearance flyout rows that need a pick stay available: a click asks you to select, and the first pick runs the command (Paste Appearance is left out when nothing was copied).
  • Menus with nothing open: the menu bar is File, View and Tools only (help is the ? button), with no Customize Menu at the foot and no Toolbox under Tools; Help, Edit, Insert, Window and Customize Menu come back when a document opens. Right-click on a model face or edge while a sketch is open shows that item's own menu: the edge or face first (Select Midpoint, Select Loop, Select Connected Faces), then selection tools, zoom/pan/rotate, Display Grid, Recent Commands, Sketch Entities, More Dimensions, Relations, Sketch Tools and Body. A page that cannot finish yet no longer greys OK in the graphics-area right-click menu or on the mouse gesture ring: the menu starts at Cancel and the ring keeps its view command on top until OK can be pressed.
  • Notification area: hidden at first. View, User Interface, Notification Area (or a right-click on the status bar) brings it back.
  • Status bar numbers: the pointer position and the measurements (Length, Diameter, Distance, Area) show the unit glued on, with the Length decimals set in Document Properties and without trailing zeros ("37.7mm", "12mm", "0mm"); angles follow the Angle decimals. A measurement never reads "-0mm".
  • Status bar: a message and FreeCAD's own pick line (Preselected ...) no longer draw over each other; the pick line sits in the right-hand group.
  • Large assemblies: Large Assembly Mode, with Optimize image quality on, draws curves a little coarser so big models turn faster.
  • Hovering: the face or edge under the pointer is outlined at once, and with a Selection Filter on, hovering an edge outlines the face behind it that the filter allows. After a short rest the feature's name (for example Boss-Extrude1) shows as a tip over faces and edges. Zooming in with the wheel keeps the spot under the pointer where it is, as zooming out does. Dragging an end face with Instant3D changes the depth live.
  • Big parts stay quick: hovering or clicking on a part with hundreds of features, turning a big assembly (the first frame of a middle-drag turn), and orbiting round faces all stay responsive. No hover outline is drawn while you turn or pan with the middle or right button, and the outline of a face seen at a slant no longer breaks along hole rims.
  • Big assemblies are much quicker: RealView on a 300-part assembly (10 s down to 0.3 s), the floor reflection (15 s to under 1 s), the picked-face highlight (50 ms to 3 ms) and the outline walk of Hidden Lines Removed (185 ms to 32 ms) all got faster. Hidden Lines Removed and Visible follow the Tangent Edges display option. An appearance dropped on an assembly component offers Component (name) with Undo, and a drop on a component's tree row shows the pop-up. Hide/Show types remember what was shown; items hidden on their own stay hidden. Previous View (Ctrl+Shift+Z) steps back through the views in order. The reference triad uses lifted shades over the dark viewport. Edit Material on a part with a material per configuration changes the configuration that is shown.
  • Task Pane and links: an unpinned Task Pane slides shut when you drag an appearance or a Design Library item out of it, so the model behind can take the drop (a pinned or floating pane stays). Links in panels (for example Open Appearance Library) are light blue in Dark and blue in Light after a theme switch, and unticked check boxes in dialogs show their box. Decals picked on an assembly component go on that component and follow it.
  • Reference geometry and Measure: Edit Feature (or a double-click) on a plane, axis, coordinate system or point opens its panel again with its references, and OK changes that same feature. A part's mate reference sits in the tree's Mate References folder and follows a rename. A click inside a plane that lies on a model face picks the face; the plane is picked at its outline. Measure uses the part's Angle decimals, measures in whichever part is active, drops picks whose face is gone and measures again after a rebuild, and a coordinate system picked as a whole, or through an assembly component, measures its origin. An overridden centre of mass given "As defined in" a coordinate system follows it when it moves.
  • Custom properties: a property name can be used once (a second one is refused with a message and the row keeps its name); Weight or Material as a name fills its value with the part's own linked value.
  • Picked faces: a face you click fills with the selection colour at any angle, including the top and sides in an Isometric view, with its edges still drawn over the fill.
  • Display styles: Hidden Lines Removed and Hidden Lines Visible show the background through the faces (they are not filled white), in a part and in an assembly, where each component gives its own edges. An assembly in Shaded hides the parts' edges, and an assembly in Wireframe draws every edge. In Dark the edges of Hidden Lines Removed, Hidden Lines Visible and Wireframe are light grey (hidden edges dashed), not black. A cylinder in Hidden Lines Removed shows both its outline lines, and Hidden Lines Removed and Visible draw no seam line down a cylinder (Shaded With Edges never did). Tangent Edges as Phantom works on parts that have a Body and a tip feature.
  • Dark 3D view: grey parts read clearly: a soft light from the left brightens the faces that turn away from the main light.
  • RealView Graphics: the toggle in View Settings shades the model per pixel with a glossy studio reflection. Metals reflect the studio in their own colour, glass turns mirror-like at a grazing angle, a low-shine finish sees a softened studio, and the reflection follows the scene you apply. It draws only in the shaded display styles (not Hidden Lines, Wireframe or Points). Appearance swatches in the task pane carry matching metal, plastic, paint, rubber, glass and stone values, and Ambient Occlusion works with it.
  • Speed: while you turn the view or hold a drag, the background work that refreshes silhouettes and status items waits until you stop, and while a file opens the add-in does not watch every object being restored. A command that fails says so on the status bar instead of staying silent. The Welcome news asks for its feed a few seconds after the dialog opens, so it never holds up the start.
  • Select Other: a small list window (an x, no title) opens with the face under the pointer already hidden, so the list starts with what is behind it. Right-click in the graphics area hides the face under the pointer and Shift+right-click shows the last hidden one. Hover a row to highlight it and click to pick it; closing the list (x, Esc or a pick) shows everything again and leaves the document's modified mark as it was.
  • Menu status lines: pointing at any menu bar item (in a part, an assembly or a drawing) shows that item's own line in the status bar, in the add-on's own words.
  • Normal To (on a tree row's icon row, and Ctrl+8) with a whole feature picked turns the view to that feature's sketch plane: a sketch or plane itself, a feature's profile sketch, a body's last feature's sketch, a structural member's first path sketch. Rows with no plane (Fillet, Gusset, End Cap, the Weldment row, bodies whose last feature has no sketch) do not show Normal To.
  • Right-click shows a short menu that fits what is under the pointer. Click » at the bottom for the full menu; menus stay full until you open a new document. Above the menu is a small icon box of the commands you use most with that item. On a feature or sketch in the tree it has two lines: Edit Feature, Edit Sketch, Rollback and Suppress, over Hide, Zoom to Selection, Normal To and Appearance. On a plane it is one line. Its pictures are small (16 pixels) so the box stays compact, and many menu items (Delete, Properties, Configure Feature, Select Other, Display Grid and more) carry a small picture too.
  • Tree right-click menus: feature and sketch rows have Suppress before Rollback in the icon row. A right-click on a model corner is on the vertex. A body under a weldment cut list item gets the Body menu (Hide, Isolate, Delete/Keep Body..., Insert into New Part..., Comment, Go To...). Menus FreeCAD leaves over are deleted a few seconds after they leave the menu bar, so a long session stays quick.
  • CommandManager: the flyouts match the flyout lists of the program this look is modelled on (Rib, Wrap, Mirror, Offset and Ruled Surface and Curvature have no flyout arrow), commands that left a flyout have a menu row, and the tab row shortens the names of its tabs when the window is narrow instead of showing a >> button. The Save modified documents box fits its buttons and opens centred on the main window.
  • CommandManager while a panel is open: the CommandManager stays live while a property panel is open; a click on a command closes the open page and starts that command. The Sketch button stays live while a sketch is open (a click exits and keeps the sketch). Plane, Axis, Point and Coordinate System can be made while a feature's panel is open, and you return to the feature. On a narrow window the >> overflow strip shows Reference Geometry, Curves and Instant3D as CommandManager buttons. The Surfaces tab's Fillet button has a Fillet and Chamfer flyout. Tangent Snap is listed in the Quick Snaps only for the tools that use it (Line, Center Arc). The assembly tab's Top Level Transparency works with one Undo step, and the button is left out when there is nothing to act on.
  • CommandManager tab menu and Customize: right-click a tab for Tabs, Use Large Buttons with Text, Show Description and Customize CommandManager. In Customize a dropped button goes where you let go it, dragging along a tab or the Shortcut Bar reorders buttons, and Reset clears your changes.
  • Welcome: the Welcome box opens tall enough for the whole Home page, at most 85% of the screen height.
  • Panels: the mouse wheel over a field in a panel scrolls the panel instead of changing the field; the list of unit suggestions that opens while you type a number sits beside the field and never covers the next one; Keep Visible reopens the panel after OK; and tables in panels are readable in Dark.

Task pane

The tabs on the right edge of the window: Home, Design Library, File Explorer, View Palette, Appearances, and Custom Properties. A click slides the pane over the view; the pin keeps it open. The pane's title row has a double chevron on the left that folds it away, the page name in bold, and a gear (Options) and the pin on the right; the tabs at the edge move between pages. Appearances has a row of Back and Forward (each shows only while it has somewhere to go), Edit Appearance, Refresh and Up One Level (below the top only), a picture on every folder row, 104 px swatches with the whole name under each, and a note under the tree; dropping an appearance on a feature tree row shows the level pop-up for that item. The swatch grid is sized from the font of the longest name, so long names such as "Glossy White Plastic" fit at larger interface fonts. In Appearances the colour folders read Plastic, Metal, Painted, Rubber, Glass, Light, Fabric, Organic, Stone and Miscellaneous (Light swatches glow), and Scenes is split into Basic, Studio and Presentation Scenes (the Scenes folder itself lists all of them). Scene folders show a small picture of each scene, a tooltip names the scene, and the tips speak of scenes. The Decals folder has its own tip. In Design Library a right-click on a folder has its own rows, and a forming tools folder follows the mark you set on it.

Model tabs and status bar

Below the view are the tabs Model, 3D Views and Motion Study. The status bar shows a prompt for the current tool on the left. In a sketch it also shows the pointer position, whether the sketch is under, fully or over defined (click the red Over Defined to fix it), and the sketch's name. The "Editing Part" or "Editing Sketch1" label stays at the right and does not move while the pointer coordinates change, and a new empty sketch reads Under Defined. Pointer coordinates never read "-0.00mm". Quick Measure shows at the right of the status bar for what you have picked: one point shows its X, Y and Z; two items show Distance with dX, dY and dZ (and Total Length for edges); parallel lines show the normal distance; lines or flat faces at an angle show Angle (for example 90deg); an arc shows Arc Length and its centre; a curve shows Length; any other face shows Area. On the right is the units box: click it to switch between the metre, centimetre, millimetre and inch systems. Custom... is ticked when the document uses another system (such as feet and inches) and opens the Units page to pick one; Edit Document Units opens the same page.

In a feature panel or a sketch, D moves the confirmation corner (and the breadcrumbs) to the pointer, so OK and Cancel are close at hand.

Opening, saving and exporting

The File menu boxes (Open, Save As, New, Print, Pack and Go and the rest) are laid out the way CAD users expect.

  • Open: the Quick Filter has picture buttons for parts, assemblies and drawings (the top-level assembly shows a small blue arrow), with the filter named in the tooltip. Use Speedpak and Do not load hidden components show only while an assembly is picked. The preview picture is sharp at any screen scale.
  • Open, Configurations and Display States: the two lists show only for a part or an assembly (not for nothing picked, a STEP file or a drawing). The Display State list shows only the states of the configuration you chose and changes when you choose another. References... shows only when the file refers to other files.
  • Open read-only lasts for this session only. File, Save then says "<file> was opened read-only" (use Save As), the Close and Exit save questions leave it out, and opening read-only does not mark the document as changed. Save As over the file clears read-only. A read-only mark left inside a file by an earlier build is removed.
  • Open that cannot open a file: a file that was moved, is damaged, is not what its name says, or has nothing to read (an empty STL has no triangles) gets one Open box that names it and says why; nothing half-opened is left, and the other files still open. A FreeCAD file whose shape data is missing inside opens with what could be read and says so ("Keep what was read with Save As under a new name"). Backup files (.FCBak, .FCStd1) open as FreeCAD files. Welcome, File, Open and the Task Pane all use the same box.
  • Where Open and Save As start: Open and the first Save As of a new part start in the folder you last opened from or saved to (the Documents folder the first time). Show previous versions (the arrow beside Open) opens the version you pick, read-only, as a dated copy. The Type column names FreeCAD files "FreeCAD Part Document", "FreeCAD Assembly Document" and "FreeCAD Drawing Document". File, Open Recent lists files opened through Open, the Welcome or Save As, newest first.
  • Save As that would overwrite an open file: Save As and Save as copy refuse to write over a file that is open in FreeCAD, also through a folder link, a mapped drive or a short (8.3) name: "X.FCStd is open in FreeCAD. Close it first, or save under a different name." Nothing is written. Save All skips a read-only document with a Report view line and saves the rest, and every save writes Last Saved By (FreeCAD's author name, else the computer's sign-in name).
  • Files open elsewhere: while a FreeCAD file is open, FreeCAD keeps a small hidden file named .~$<name>.lck beside it and removes it when the file closes (you see it only with hidden files shown). A file that is open elsewhere opens read-only, with a File In Use box. Save asks before it overwrites a file that changed on disk after you opened it; the default answer is No.
  • Files that cannot be opened safely: a damaged .FCStd is refused, or opens read-only with a box, so Save cannot write over it; a file that needs a workbench that is not installed says so and names it, instead of calling the file damaged; a document brought back by Document Recovery opens normally, not read-only. A file saved by a newer version opens read-only with a box, and a document with the same name from another folder is not opened beside one that is already open. Recent Documents opens STEP, IGES and STL files too, and files dropped on the window open through File, Open (no STEP Import Settings box). Opening a file made by another CAD program's own part or assembly format gives one clear box that says what to do (export it as STEP there). The Open dialog's Type column shows "FreeCAD Assembly Document" in full.
  • Two FreeCAD windows: the temporary files the add-on writes (Smart Fasteners, Evaluate, mass properties, comments and others) are named per session, so two FreeCAD windows open at once do not overwrite each other's.
  • Exports: an assembly that uses the same part twice exports every instance, where it sits, to IGES, STL, 3MF, OBJ, AMF and VRML (a file for each component is named after the instance, such as Asm-MmPart_1.stl). An export does not mark the document as changed, so closing does not ask to save. A part saved as a picture is the size of the graphics area, even when its window is behind another. A STEP or IGES body is named Imported1, Imported2... and the Run Import Diagnostics question uses that name. A mesh file (STL, OBJ, OFF, PLY) has no unit: Options, Import, Mesh body options, Unit says what its numbers mean (Millimeters at first), and the Report view says which unit was used. Exporting a new part with no solid or surface body says "X was not saved: Part1 has no solid or surface body to export yet."
  • STEP and IGES in and out: IGES Output as Manifold Solid (type 186) is applied to each export (it used to need a restart). The STEP of an assembly names its products after the parts (Plate, Pin), not after their bodies. A STEP assembly opens as an assembly: each part once (a body with its Imported1 feature, colours kept), a component for every place it sits ([Pin^Asm]<1>, [Pin^Asm]<2>), and a sub-assembly for each sub-assembly; it opens up to date, and Save Part (in External File) gives a part its own file. An inch STEP or IGES opens as an inch part, a millimetre one in millimetres.
  • Decimals belong to the part: Document Properties, Units, Decimals are kept in each part: .123 in one part leaves the others as they are, comes back when that part's window is active again, and is saved with the file. A new part starts with its template's .12. A file saved before this keeps what it showed.
  • Picking with a lasso and from the tree: a lasso drawn clockwise picks what lies wholly inside it, anticlockwise what it touches. Clicking a feature in the tree picks that feature, and clicking one of its faces in the view picks its row in the tree.
  • References and missing files: a part that moved or was renamed shows File Not Found with Browse, Suppress and Skip, and only that (no rebuild question first). A drawing whose model moved says "The model of X was not found. Browse to it?" and Yes puts the views back on the file you pick in one undo step. Find References shows the stored folder and lists components broken since the last save.
  • Configurations in assemblies and parts: switching an assembly's configuration rebuilds the component sizes it changes. Deleting a part configuration that an instance used shows the active one, with a Report view line. The ConfigurationManager's rows have icons (a folder for Tables, a table icon for the design table, and a configuration icon that is in colour for the active one); inactive configurations keep normal text.
  • File Properties: Summary has a Statistics group (Created, Last Saved, Last Saved By, Last Saved With). Properties Summary lists the property names on the left and, for the one picked, a grid of Configuration Name, Type, Value / Text Expression and Evaluated Value.
  • Open and Save As files: the Open box and the Save As box (a drawing's Save As too) open at about 55 percent of the screen width and 62 percent of its height and keeps the size you give it (Open and Save As remember their sizes apart), with the Name, Size, Type and Date Modified columns wide enough to read whole. Save As of a FreeCAD file or a template puts it at the top of Recent Documents at once; exports are not listed.
  • Open, Options...: the Open box has an Options... button right of File type when the type has import options. STEP, IGES, ACIS and Parasolid open System Options, Import at that file format; STL, OBJ, 3MF, AMF, PLY, OFF and VRML open it at Mesh. It follows the File type you pick or the file you pick (All readable files with a .stl file picked shows it for Mesh). For FreeCAD files, All Files and a mixed pick it is hidden.
  • Save All: saves every document that has changes (or was never saved), the ones other documents refer to first, so parts go before the assemblies that use them. It does not stop at a Save Modified Documents box; Ctrl+S on one assembly whose parts changed still asks.
  • Save as copy and continue leaves the copy out of Recent Documents (the copy is not opened); Save as copy and open lists it.
  • Save As: Options... shows only for a file type that has options. Add prefix and Add suffix show once Include all referenced components is ticked; they are a pair of radio buttons sharing one text field.
  • Export Options: Save As, Options... opens the Export Options box on the type you picked, with a File Format list (IGES, STEP, STL, 3MF) and Help. For STEP, Output as AP203, AP214 or AP242 (picking one switches the Save as type), Export face/edge properties (off keeps no colours), 3D curves, sketch entities, trim accuracy and split periodic faces. For STL, the deviation and angle boxes show only with Custom, and Preview only under Show STL info. Types with no options (VRML, OBJ, glTF and pictures) have no Options button. System Options, Export has the same Export face/edge properties row for STEP, and Output as (the STEP types this FreeCAD writes; hidden if there is only one). That choice is FreeCAD's own STEP scheme setting, applied at once, so File, Export writes that type, and Save As, Options... opens on it. Save As with the type STEP AP214 writes AP214 even when the setting is AP203: each Save As STEP type writes its own type and leaves the setting as it was.
  • New (Advanced): the template tabs have Large Icons, List and List Details buttons at the top right (your choice is remembered; List Details shows each template's file, size and date). A kind of document this FreeCAD cannot make is left out of the box instead of being greyed. Make Assembly from Part with the prompt for a template shows only the assembly tile.
  • Document Recovery: if FreeCAD ended without closing (a crash), the next start shows no recovery box in front of the window. The Document Recovery page opens by itself instead, on the Task Pane when a view is open, otherwise in a panel at the right of the window. It lists each document with its time and Not recovered or Recovered. Open gives the unsaved work back as the document, on its own file, marked as changed. A document that was never saved comes back with no file, so Save asks for a name (it does not write into the recovery folder). The panel goes away when nothing is left to recover. What you did not recover is still offered the next time, and stock FreeCAD without the add-on offers it too.
  • Save As, errors and units: after every Save As of a FreeCAD file, STEP, IGES, PDF, 3MF, glTF, STL or VRML the file is looked for, and a missing one is reported. The error says first, in plain words, what to check (the folder can be written to; the file is not open in another program: a file locked on Windows lands here), then the details. STEP, IGES and DXF are written in the document's own units (an inch part gives an inch STEP and IGES with inch numbers, and the DXF header says inches); millimetre documents are written as before. A STEP of a single-body part names its product after the part file. Save as copy and open with an export type (STEP, IGES, STL...) opens the exported copy through the matching importer, named after the file. A part made of plain shapes (no Body) that is used in an assembly exports again.
  • Part Save As, Dxf: for any part, Save As with the type Dxf opens the DXF / DWG Output panel. Annotation views lists the Front, Back, Left, Right, Top, Bottom and Current views (each name starts with a star), drawn from the model with no drawing needed, plus any drawing views of the part; the Front view is ticked. Hidden edges adds the hidden lines on the HIDDEN layer, dashed. Several views go side by side in one file, or into Separate files. A part that is not sheet metal has no Sheet metal option (it is hidden); a sheet metal part keeps it, and the Entities To Export box and Output Alignment show only where they apply. A flat face picked before Save As starts the panel in Faces / loops / edges with that face listed. OK opens the DXF/DWG Cleanup window named after the part: Save writes the file, Cancel writes nothing. The version, fonts and the other output settings come from System Options, Export, DXF/DWG (chapter 8). Right-click a flat face, Export to DXF / DWG, does the same: the file name starts as the part's name in the part's folder, and the DXF / DWG Output panel opens with the face listed under Faces / loops / edges. A curved face still goes straight to a file.
  • File > Import and Export types: each file type is listed only when the program that reads or writes it is there, so no entry is a dead end. With the file reader that comes with the add-on you get the part and assembly files of one mainstream CAD system and Parasolid (to open). A converter that is not included adds Inventor, CATIA V5, Fusion 360, Rhino, Creo View and ACIS (and Rhino export). The message for a type FreeCAD cannot open names FreeCAD, not the add-on. The types of that CAD system's own files are listed as Part, Assembly and Part and Assembly, each with its file extensions, in Open and in Save As.
  • Open: Open is one split button; its arrow, inside the button on the right, has Open Read-only and Show previous versions. New (Advanced): the lines on the List and List Details buttons follow the theme's text colour, so they show on Dark.
  • Pack and Go: the file list is a Nested view by default (each file sits under the closest document that uses it, with drawings at the top) or a Flat view. Each file has a tick box; files you untick are not packed. Include simulation results off leaves the results out of the packed copy only: your original and the open document stay as they are. An assembly's subassembly links are written so they still resolve after Save As into another folder and in the packed copy, which no longer says "File Not Found" for its subassemblies.
  • Pack and Go and Include drawings: every drawing of every packed file is found: open drawings that show any of them, and drawing files in their folders that refer to them under any name. A drawing that is not open goes beside its model for Flatten to minimal folders and Keep full folder paths. Two files that would be saved to one path (two Bracket.FCStd from different folders flattened) show in red; Save names the file and writes nothing until one is renamed.
  • Find References has Show nested view and Help. Its Print... keeps the nesting.
  • Print: for a part or an assembly the Print range shows All only; the Sheets field is always live, and typing in it picks Sheets. Print Preview has Zoom In, Zoom Out and Help. Print Selection shows its area rows only with Selection. Page Setup and Summary Information have Help.
  • Modify Configurations (Configure Feature, Configure Dimension) in an inch part shows lengths in inches and takes a typed number in inches ("4" is 4 in); a fraction (1/2), an equation (=2*1.5) and a comma decimal (1,5) typed bare are in the part's unit, an equation stays an equation, a value with its own unit (25.4 mm) is taken as written, and a cell you did not type over keeps its exact value. Angles, counts, global variables and millimetre parts are unchanged. A change made to another configuration in the table rebuilds each configuration it changed, as one Undo step; another configuration's Material, Density and Mass are worked out from its material (its Configure Material cell, else the part's) and its Override Mass Properties when they are read.
  • Configuration properties: renaming a configuration moves its own properties (File Properties, Configuration Properties), their units and its comment, Use in BOM and colour with it; deleting one removes them; copy and paste of a configuration keeps each property's type (Number, Date and so on) and units. A drawing's Properties have no Configuration Properties tab. Undo and Redo of a rename or delete bring them back too.
  • Custom Properties: the configuration list shows with Configuration specific. A property linked to another configuration's mass reads that configuration's own Mass, Volume, Density, Material, Surface Area and Center of Mass: each configuration's values are kept when it is shown or left, so Long's weight does not read the value of the configuration that happens to be shown. A property linked to the mass reads in the part's own mass unit (g, kg or lb).
  • Import Diagnostics: Attempt to Heal All shows only when there is something to heal. It joins faces that sit a hair apart (0.0004 mm, common in STEP and IGES files from other systems) into one solid: if the plain sew does not do it, it tries a fuzzy join at 0.001 mm, then sews, and keeps the plain result whenever the join is not better.

Themes

Options, Colors offers Light, Medium Light, Medium, Dark and a pure black theme (the Medium Light and Medium colours are close estimates, not exact). Medium Light and Medium use the Light layout with a darker window and menu colour. Light and Dark draw a soft scene behind the part: Light is white at the top left, blue-grey towards the upper right, darkest just above a soft horizon about 60 percent of the way down, with a white floor below; Dark has the same shape in dark greys. The pure black theme and other scenes keep FreeCAD's plain gradient. The scene fills the view, cannot be picked and is ignored by Zoom to Fit. In Light the title row and the ribbon are the same pale grey, the active tab is lighter, and the picked row in the feature tree is a softer blue. Dark changes the window, and keeps list and value boxes white so values stay easy to read. In Dark, radio buttons everywhere (for example the sketch tools' Orientation box) show a light ring with a blue dot on the picked one, and the tree's icons for items that are not shown, such as the default planes and Origin, are drawn at full strength, not faded. In Dark and the pure black theme, icons are drawn light, including the ones FreeCAD loads by its own names (the tree and the commands), so none is black on a dark pane. After switching, the add-on's icons are ready at once and FreeCAD's own are converted in the background and ready from the next start. Drawing sheet tabs follow the theme too.
