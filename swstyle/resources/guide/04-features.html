4. Features

Features turn sketches into solids and change solids. They are on the Features tab and in the Insert menu. Each one opens a property panel with a yellow preview; Enter makes the feature as one undo step.

Tips

  • An open sketch ticks Thin Feature for you.
  • Drag the arrow in the preview to change a depth; a ruler appears for exact values.
  • If a feature can't be made, the panel stays open and says why.

Edges and faces

FeaturePanel highlights
FilletFour kinds (constant size, variable size, face fillet, full round). Pick edges, faces, features or a whole body; the size can be typed before picking. Tangent propagation, preview level, round corners

Picking a body first and then Fillet or Chamfer takes all its edges. A radius or distance you type and confirm with the green check at once is the one used (it is no longer lost). FilletXpert's Add tab has an Options box (Select through faces, Tangent propagation) that matches the Manual tab, and the value suggestion list closes with its page.

  • Partial Edge Parameters: with one edge picked, set a Start and an End condition, each None, Distance (mm) or Percentage (%) with its value. Flip edge direction measures from the other end. The fillet runs only between the two offsets and the rest of the edge stays sharp. With several edges picked, a note asks for one edge and the fillet is whole.
  • Variable Size Fillet, Straight transition: with only a start and an end radius the radius changes in a straight line along the edge: 2 mm to 5 mm reads 2.75, 3.5 and 4.25 mm at a quarter, half and three quarters. Smooth transition is unchanged. A variable fillet saved without a Transition keeps the straight transition.

Copies and patterns

Linear Pattern, Circular Pattern, Curve Driven, Sketch Driven, Table Driven, Fill and Variable Pattern, and Mirror. Pick the features, faces or bodies to copy, then the direction, axis or plane. "Instances to Skip" lets you click copies off in the preview.

Reference geometry

Plane (up to three references, each coincident, parallel, perpendicular, offset, at an angle, mid plane or tangent), Axis, Coordinate System, Point, and Mate Reference. The panel says "Fully defined" once the plane has enough references: the message bar turns green. References are named as you would expect (Point1@Sketch4, Arc1@Sketch4, Face<1>), and Flip normal sits in its own Options group. While a plane is not yet defined it shows as a yellow outline with a blue normal; once defined it previews see-through blue-grey. Flip normal also works before the first pick, and ticking it first applies it to the first reference. In Point, with Define position with numeric values on, the Selections box and the point type buttons step aside. Projected Curve, Sketch on sketch, has one box, Sketches to Project, that takes the two sketches; Sketch on faces keeps Sketch to Project and Projection Faces; Edit Feature loads them again.

Bounding Box: it still works when the part already has a Center of Mass point, another box or a curve (those do not consume the body). OK adds Total Bounding Box Length, Width, Thickness and Volume to each configuration's own properties (File Properties, Configuration Specific), as plain numbers linked to the box. The box follows the part when it rebuilds, and Best Fit also tries the part's biggest flat faces, so a turned part is boxed tight.

Surfaces, direct editing and more

The Surfaces tab has extruded, revolved, swept, lofted, boundary, planar, filled, offset, ruled and knit surfaces, plus trim, extend and thicken. Panel names: Knit Surface (Gap Control is ticked for a new knit; the knitting tolerance is 0.0025 mm and Show gaps in range runs 0.0025 mm to 0.1 mm, with 4 decimals; parts saved before keep their settings), Offset Surface and Fill Surface (its list is "Edge settings:"; for a new fill Apply to all edges is unticked and Optimize surface is ticked; the options read Create solid and Reverse direction; Fix up boundary and Merge result stay ticked); Replace Face's group is "Replace Parameters". Extruded Surface with nothing picked opens the Extrude page that asks for a sketch, or a plane or flat face to sketch on; a sketch picked opens Surface-Extrude on it, and a plane or face starts a sketch, with Surface-Extrude following when the sketch closes. Swept and Lofted Boss/Base and Cuts with nothing picked make the feature with no profile yet and open its full panel (OK and X, Profile and Path or Profiles, Options, Guide Curves), with the Profile box taking the first pick; Cancel takes it away. Surface fixes: Untrim Surface on a trimmed planar surface goes back to the parent surface's outline (a 25 x 20 trim of a 40 x 20 surface comes back as 40 x 20); Exclude parent surfaces keeps the Distance % growth. Planar Surface absorbs and hides its sketch, so a click on the surface's edge in Extend Surface picks the surface, not the sketch. Extend Surface (Same surface) on a planar face gives one face. Replace Face takes a whole surface body as the replacement surface, picked from the tree or by a face of the body. Thicken of a knitted surface with a sharp outside corner falls back to sharp joins instead of failing. The Direct Editing tab has move, delete and replace face, scale, dome, indent, flex, split line and intersect. Modify box on a feature's own dimension (an extrude's depth, say): when the value is linked or driven by an equation, typing a new number changes the linked value so every dimension on it follows, and the box opens with the equation when one drives it. Extend Surface has three groups, and Planar Surface's boundary group is titled Bounding Entities. Surface-Loft takes 2 to 100 sections (12 at first). In a part with several bodies, a boss or cut with Merge result off puts the new body where the feature placed it (a box placed away from the origin, or a pad on an offset sketch, lands where you put it). Intersect: click a face of one of the part's own solid bodies to pick that body. On a box, cylinder, sphere or cone, Merge result off keeps the new solid as its own body in a second body. The bodies you pick cut each other into regions (two overlapping bodies make three regions) and the part's other bodies stay.

Edge picks work with seam edges hidden: a click on an edge after a hidden seam or tangent edge picks that edge, not the one before. Filled Surface on a hole cut by Trim Surface takes the whole hole loop (with Fix up boundary on), an open or empty boundary stops with "The patch boundary must be a closed loop of edges", and Tangent or Curvature on the end of a loft domes out and holds the tangency. Knit and Thicken take the whole surface when you click a surface body (a face of a solid stays one face), Ruled Surface works on a closed edge, and Delete/Keep Body can pick a surface body even when the part has a solid. A new Flex starts with its triad square to the model axes, Z along the Front Plane normal. A guided loft keeps each original profile edge (a line stays a line), so a square loft is six clean faces and Shell works on it. Wrap Scribe keeps every body of the part. The Fillet panel opens at its default radius on a shelled flange without hanging. Combo boxes in panels have a thin chevron. Swept Cut with a Solid Profile tool takes a solid outside the part as the tool (a ball touching the part now cuts). In a new part, Boundary Cut, Fillet, Chamfer, Draft, Shell, Wrap and Intersect are not greyed: each opens a message page that says it works on a solid, with Extruded Boss/Base and Revolved Boss/Base buttons that start the boss. Knit Surface: Create solid is always available; ticked on surfaces that do not close a volume, the page says so in red. With Gap Control off, the knitting tolerance and gap range fields are hidden. Parting Surface groups its options as Mold Parameters, Parting Surface (Distance, Reverse alignment, and Angle only for the two surface-following types) and Options. Flex works on parts with a dome, a sphere or a drill point; before, it refused those. Thicken on a face of a surface body thickens the whole body. Hole Wizard Options name the countersink rows Near Side, Under Head and Far Side Countersink Diameter and Angle, and Curve Driven Pattern, Fill Pattern and Instances to Vary use plain labels (Pattern Direction, Instances per loop, Direction 1 and 2 depth increment); their hints are in the tooltips.

Feature panel layout: a few panels were tidied to match the other ones. Boss-Extrude and Cut-Extrude: with Direction 2 unticked its list (Blind) is not shown at all, and the Draft On/Off button sits at the left of its row; with draft off the angle and Draft outward are not shown, with draft on the angle sits beside the button and Draft outward under it. Linear Pattern's Direction 2 has its tick in its title and starts folded and off, as Circular Pattern's does; picking a direction or Symmetric ticks it. Sweep: Merge tangent faces and Show preview are in Options, before Merge result, and Align with end faces comes after it; Direction 1, Bidirectional and Direction 2 show only when the path runs both ways from the profile. Loft: Merge tangent faces, Close loft, Ruled surface, Show preview, Merge result and Micro tolerance are in Options, and Move Up and Move Down under the Profiles box reorder them (the first is the Profile, the rest are Sections; Cancel puts the order back). Shell and Draft open with no message line; Draft's Manual and DraftXpert tabs sit at the top of the page. On the Surfaces tab, Surface-Extrude and Surface-Revolve have Direction 2 ticked in the title (folded to begin with) and the Draft On/Off button; Surface-Revolve asks for the axis in a pink box, not a red band. Knit Surface has Gap Control ticked in its title. Every Surfaces panel is packed to the top of the pane, with no blank bands between or under its rows.

Sheet metal

The Design Library's forming tools folder has embosses, extruded flanges, lances, louvers and ribs, each tool a part in its own category folder (an older flat file moves into its category the first time it is used).

Base Flange/Tab's Bend Allowance and Auto Relief title check boxes work: unticked, the sheet metal defaults are used and the rows are hidden. Custom Bend Allowance, Custom Relief Type and Corner Treatment have header check boxes the same way, and the Closed Corner "match" box is always shown. Sheet Metal Gusset has its own on/off toggle for Side face draft, Inner corner fillet and Outer corner fillet, with the value beside it; a value switched off is hidden and the gusset is built without it (the toggles show for a flat gusset only, and gussets from before keep their shape). Trim/Extend has a Preview check box (on and remembered: every change trims at once; off: picks and options are kept and the members are trimmed at OK). An Edge Flange page is titled with the flange's name, Edge-Flange1. A new Edge Flange has Trim side bends on and a new Hem opens as Open. Sheet metal panels hide a box that does not apply and switch its option on when you use a live one: Edge Flange (Edit Flange Profile with one edge picked, angle and face angle modes, length end condition, Multi-length, Use relief ratio), Miter Flange, Hem, Jog, Sketched Bend, Closed Corner (gap ratio and narrow corner rows hidden for Butt) and Tab and Slot. Typing a radius or K-Factor turns Use default radius off or Custom Bend Allowance on. The first Base Flange stores its bend radius (Base-Flange1.Radius) and Edge Flange's Use default radius reads it. A second Base Flange/Tab takes the part's thickness (the thickness boxes are hidden, not greyed). A Base Flange made any way (the panel, the SheetMetal workbench's own command, or a macro) gets its Sheet-Metal, Cut list and Flat-Pattern folders from the first Base Flange on, and one Undo (or Cancel) takes the flange and its folders away together. Sheet Metal Sketch on the Sheet Metal tab does what Sketch does: with nothing picked it opens the Edit Sketch page that asks for a plane or face. Start with Base Flange/Tab on a sketch, then add Edge Flange, Miter Flange, Hem, Jog, Sketched Bend, Lofted Bend, corners and reliefs. Flatten shows the flat pattern with bend lines; click it again to fold. On a part that has a sheet metal body, Fold says to unfold some bends first (Unfold button), No Bends says it works on parts made with Insert Bends (Insert Bends button) and Update Unfold says to unfold with the add-on's Unfold first; with no sheet metal body they still say to make one. Picture buttons (the name is in the tooltip) replace radio lists for Trim/Extend Corner Type, the Structural Member corner treatment, End Cap thickness direction, Gusset profile, thickness side and location, and Edge Flange's Flange Length and Flange Position. The angle boxes on Cross Break, Forming Tool, Gusset, Stamp, Vent and Lofted Bend read "deg". Every pick box draws a pick as Edge<1> (numbered per kind). In Tab and Slot each pick box starts with its picture, the active box is light blue, and the groups read Group1. The Bounding-Box (the grey dash-dot rectangle round the outline) stays hidden until you show it; if you show it while the part is flat, the next Flatten shows it again. Flatten also works on a part with an Edge Flange and a Closed Hem on the same side. The Forming Tool panel keeps the part on screen while you pick faces. Jog measures its height to the moved face nearest the jog line, so a Sketched Bend lip at the far end no longer gives "offset too small". The tree shows Sheet-Metal right after the Origin and Flat-Pattern last. A sheet metal button in a part with no sheet metal (Cross-Break, Swept Flange, Corners, Corner Relief, Forming Tool, Gusset, Stamp, Vent, Normal Cut, Bend Notch, Unfold, Fold, Flatten, No Bends, Update Unfold) is not greyed: it opens its panel saying "Make a sheet metal body first", with Base Flange/Tab and Convert to Sheet Metal buttons, and the command starts once a sheet exists.

Weldments

In the Structural Member panel the message reads "Select a profile type and size for the Structural Member." until a path segment is picked, then "Select sketch segments for defining path. You can rotate the profile by a specified angle."; the Settings group appears once there is a path segment, and Apply corner treatment with its three buttons comes before Merge miter trimmed bodies. In Settings, Simple cut cuts a butting member's end flat on the face it meets, and Coped cut (the default for a new group) fits the end to the outline of the member it meets, so it follows a tube's rounded corners or a pipe's round side. They show at End Butt corners, or once there are two or more groups. Allow protrusion (on by default, at End Butt corners) lets the through member run past the end of its path to cover the butting member; off, it stops at the end of its path segment. A group saved before this keeps the flat cut and the protrusion. An End Butt corner on a round member (pipe, round bar) now stops flat on its side instead of losing the member. A new weldment Gusset starts with the Triangular profile (existing gussets keep theirs). End Cap's Offset is two choices, Thickness Ratio or Offset Value, each with its value under it; the value not in use is hidden. Weldment marks the part as a frame. Structural Member sweeps a standard profile (ISO and ANSI tubes, angles, channels and beams) along sketch lines, with mitred or butt corners. Add trim and extend, end caps, gussets and weld beads. The cut list groups identical members and lists length, angles, quantity and mass.

In the Weld Bead panel the two pairs of choices (Weld Geometry or Weld Path; Fillet Bead or Groove Bead) are separate groups, so the panel opens with one of each ticked and picking a bead type no longer changes the Weld Path choice. A sheet metal part's Cut list row is drawn in black, like a weldment's, not grey like a hidden feature. In the 3D view each weld path has a drawn weld symbol: an arrow from the bead to a bend, then a level reference line. The weld symbol sits on the line, with the arrow side below it and the other side above it (shifted for Staggered); fillet, square, V, bevel, U, J, flare-V, flare-bevel, plug, spot, seam and edge each have their own shape. The size comes before the symbol, the length and pitch after it, the contour (flush, convex, concave) over it, the all-around circle and the field weld flag sit at the bend and the process is in the tail. The symbol faces the screen and keeps its size in pixels as you turn and zoom, is drawn on top of the model and hides with the bead; there is one per weld path, at that path's first bead (a part saved before this gets it at the next rebuild). The cut list box is titled Cut-List Properties and its columns open wide enough to read the headings.

Editing and fixing

  • Edit Feature: double-click the feature in the tree or on the model, or pick it and use the pop-up toolbar.
  • Edit Sketch: right-click the feature, Edit Sketch.
  • Suppress and Unsuppress turn a feature off and on without deleting it.
  • Rollback: drag the blue bar up the tree to work earlier in the history. Features below the bar are not rebuilt while it is there, and Roll Back and Roll to End are several times faster than before.
  • Freeze bar: features above it are locked and won't rebuild. With Enable Freeze bar on, the rollback bar stops at the Freeze bar: the drag stops there, Rollback is not offered on a frozen feature, Roll to Previous is not offered when it would go above it, and any other route is refused with a note on the status bar.
  • What's Wrong: a feature with a red mark lists its problem in plain words, for example a face it needed has gone. Editing it offers to repair the reference.
  • Delete: one dialog lists everything that depends on the item, with Yes, Yes to All, No and Cancel.
  • Rebuild: Ctrl+B rebuilds what changed, Ctrl+Q rebuilds everything.
  • Editing a Fillet or Chamfer keeps the values you typed, and the whole edit is one undo step. Open a Fillet from the tree and the panel shows its own settings.
  • Fillet by feature: with the Fillet panel open, click a feature in the flyout tree and only the edges that feature made are rounded; click it again to take them out. Picking the body still means every edge.
  • Draft on a parting line that climbs (it is not square to the direction of pull) turns the wall about the parting edge itself, so the edge stays on the part and the wall leans exactly the draft angle.
  • Values that follow a configuration: if a depth, angle, Dome size or Fillet radius is set per configuration, typing a new value in the feature's panel changes it in the configuration you are showing; the other configurations keep their values, and Cancel leaves all of them as they were. Extrude, Cut and Revolve pages work the same way.
  • Patterns of a filleted feature: a Linear or Circular Pattern of a feature together with its fillet copies the filleted feature. The Dome panel has one box titled Parameters.
  • Mounting Boss and Snap Hook go where you clicked on the face (the click point is kept, also for a face picked before the command) unless you pick a vertex or a round edge for the position. Snap Hook Groove takes the hook when you click the hook, or the body that holds the one Snap Hook; clicking the same hook again takes it out. A "-" in an Extrude or Cut depth, or an angle over 360 in Revolve, shows a balloon at the field ("Please enter a number greater than or equal to ... and less than or equal to ...") and the key is refused. A Fillet whose radius reaches right across a block now builds.
  • Lip/Groove set to Type Groove is named Groove1, Groove2 and so on, not Lip. Delete Face is named DeleteFace1 and so on.
  • Hole features wait for their pick without showing a red mark. Cancel on a Linear, Circular or Mirror feature made from a specification leaves nothing behind. Thicken has a Parameters box. A Circular Pattern of a lofted blade (elliptical or spline sections) builds.
  • Surface bodies only: Mirror and Delete/Keep Body work in a part that holds only surface bodies (Mirror offers Bodies to Mirror).
  • Shell on a part with a Dome makes a valid solid. Reorder keeps the picks a feature already had.
  • Surface Offset offsets each shell or loose face on its own, so a knit of two shells works. Fillet opens the Fillet panel for surface bodies too: from the Surfaces tab with surface edges picked, in a part of surfaces only, from Features, Fillet with surface edges picked, and when you edit a Constant Size surface fillet. It offers Constant Size and a type that rounds two surfaces together (picking faces opens that page); what the surface fillet cannot do (FilletXpert, Variable Size, Full Round, Multi Radius, Profile, Setback, Partial Edge, Tangent propagation, round corners) is left off the panel rather than shown dead. One Undo takes it back. With no surface edge picked in a part that has a solid, it opens the solid Fillet panel.
  • Picks on a moved body: a plane, axis, point or coordinate system, a pattern direction, a mirror plane or a Hole Wizard position picked on another body that has its own placement (or a moved body in a part) lands where you picked.
  • Thread Location: the Thread panel has the Edge, an Optional start location (click the box, then pick a vertex, edge, plane or flat face and the thread starts there), an Offset check box that turns the offset distance on, Reverse direction and Start angle. A thread made before keeps its offset. In Specification, Locate Profile zooms the view to where the thread starts. The Edge, Optional start location and Up To boxes have a right-click Delete and Clear Selections.
  • Toolbox: button head screws stop at M16 and countersunk head screws at M20 (ANSI Inch is unchanged); DIN hex bolts and nuts use DIN's own across-flats and nut heights (ISO and ANSI Metric keep the ISO sizes); Configure Component, Smart Fasteners and the Design Library drop list the lengths the standard gives for the chosen size, not every length for every size, and changing the size refills the list and keeps the nearest length (the ranges are an approximation of the standards' tables). In the Smart Fasteners panel Edit Grouping shows only while a fastener row is picked in Results. Metric sizes run M3 to M24, and ANSI Inch has no retaining rings yet.
  • Extrude, Revolve, Sweep and Loft refuse a bad result when you click the green check: a profile or section that crosses itself, a feature that makes no solid, or a path tighter than the profile. A message says so and nothing is added.
  • Swept Boss/Base with Circular Profile: pick the path sketch first and it goes into the Path box (Circular Profile has no Profile box), so Enter or the green check can finish at once.
  • Swept Boss/Cut with a guide curve: with the profile and path in, the next sketch or edge you click goes into Guide Curves, and the group opens to show it. The guide narrows the sweep along its length.
  • Picking in feature panels: Shell takes a click on a lofted part's face (the thickness handle does not take the press), Freeform and Replace Face work on surface bodies, and a prompt that asks for a pick ("Please specify...") is yellow while a wrong pick's message stays red. Fillet no longer opens What's Wrong. A feature that cannot rebuild says why in plain words, and the What's Wrong panel fits its pane. Tooling Split takes Enter without an error.
  • More feature fixes: Filled Surface with Merge result: a click on the surface's own preview picks its input edge or face. Hole Wizard Tapered Tap with Through All is a blind depth just past the far side. Vertex chamfer takes a click near a corner. A click within 6 pixels of the end of an edge picks the vertex when every open box takes points only. In a pattern panel Esc cancels only the handle drag, and the Linear Pattern spacing arrow stays on the part. Weldments: a coped End Butt uses the whole tube end, and End Cap Internal on a solid bar says it cannot. Design Study has a Parameters table (Tools, Design Study, Parameters, or the Evaluate Design Study flyout).
  • Units: reference planes, axes, points, coordinate systems and 3D curves (Helix, Curve Through XYZ Points) take and show lengths in the part's own unit; an inch part shows inches. Values are kept in millimetres inside. The panels' labels, rows marked (mm) and read-only result boxes show the part's unit as well, and a length box takes a bare number in that unit or a unit you type (25mm, 1in).
  • Insert Part is live in a part with no body yet: choose it in a new part and it makes the first body.
  • Derive Part: File, Derive Part... (with a part open) makes a new part from the open one: the new part's first feature is the open part inserted and linked, its body is built on it, and the panel opens for the Transfer choices. A part that was never saved is saved first, because the link needs a file. Save Bodies (Insert, Features, and the Solid Bodies folder's Save Bodies or Create Assembly) now writes derived parts: each new part keeps a link to its body in the original and follows it on rebuild. With Consume cut bodies on they stay plain copies. An inserted or derived part follows its original: save the original again and the new part is marked to rebuild. When you open a derived part whose original changed while both were closed, it is updated from the original's file. If the original's file cannot be found, the derived part keeps its last shape, its feature reads "name->?" and one box says which file is missing; a rebuild keeps the shape too. When the file is back, the feature reads "name->" again and the part follows it.
  • Sewing tolerance: the steps that join surfaces into a solid or a shell (Knit, Thicken, loft and boundary surfaces, Fill, Merge in Untrim, Deform and the rest) now use the tolerance you give. Faces that are up to that distance apart are joined; faces further apart stay apart. Before, FreeCAD's own sewing joined only faces that touched.
  • Zero values: a dimension changed to a value that leaves a line with no length or a circle with no radius is refused (the "Changing this value would result in invalid geometry" box): Enter drops the step and Rebuild puts the earlier value back.
  • Hole Wizard Feature Scope: in a part with two or more bodies, the Type tab shows a Feature Scope group below Tolerance/Precision: All bodies, or Selected bodies with Auto-select, or the bodies you tick in the list. The hole cuts only the chosen bodies. With one body the group is hidden. Edit Feature shows the hole's scope and changes it as one undo step. A new hole starts at All bodies.
  • Boundary Boss/Base and Cut, Thin Feature: the Thin Feature fields show only while it is ticked; Reverse Direction shows only for a one-sided wall and Thickness 2 only for Two-Direction. The wall goes outward by default (Reverse Direction puts it inside), Two-Direction makes Thickness 1 outward and Thickness 2 inward, and outer corners stay sharp. A file made earlier with a one-direction thin Boundary Boss rebuilds on the other side of the profile: tick Reverse Direction to get the old wall back. In the Curvature Display group, Mesh density shows only with Mesh preview on, and Direction 1 and 2, Scale and Density only with Curvature combs on. Undo Sketch Drag shows only when there is a drag to undo.
  • Loft start and end constraint, Draft angle: works on profiles with sharp corners, and the loft leaves the profile at exactly the angle you typed.
  • Rib stops at the part: the rib's end and bottom meet the walls and floor and never go into or past them.
  • Legacy Hole shows its section picture and the Section Dimensions again.
  • Undo: each feature, edit or command is one undo step, named after what it did. If a command stops with an error, nothing half-made is left behind, so the next Undo takes back the right step.

Picking faces: a box that takes only faces (Face Fillet, Shell, Draft, Dome, Mirror, Delete Face, Move Face, Up To Surface and others) takes the face under the pointer even when you click right beside one of its edges.

Evaluate

The Evaluate tab has Measure, Mass Properties (with the centre of mass shown in the view), Section Properties, Check, Interference, Draft Analysis, Undercut, Thickness, Zebra Stripes, Curvature and Deviation. Each opens as a panel on the left like any feature.

At the right end of the tab sit nine small text buttons in three stacks of three: the simulation wizard, a flow check and a manufacturability check; a rule-based design button, On Demand Manufacturing and a sustainability check; Previous Release Check, Part Reviewer and Costing. The simulation wizard appears only here, not a second time on the tab. A button that needs something the document does not have, such as a solid, is greyed out. What the ones with a working command do:

  • Simulation wizard: a linear static study in steps (fixtures, loads, material, run, results, optimize).
  • Manufacturability check: looks at holes, inside corners and walls.
  • Sustainability check: the carbon, energy, air and water impact of the material and the making.
  • Costing: what the part costs to make, per part and per batch.
  • Part Reviewer: steps through the part's features one at a time.
  • Previous Release Check: lists what stock FreeCAD could not rebuild.
  • On Demand Manufacturing: packs STEP, STL and the part for a quote.

Zebra Stripes shows straight, parallel stripes on flat faces and bands that bend with the shape on curved ones. Where two faces meet, stripes that run on without a step mean the faces are smoothly joined. Set the number, width and direction of the stripes in its panel.
