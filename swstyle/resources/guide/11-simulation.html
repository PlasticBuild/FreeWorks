11. Simulation walkthrough

This chapter takes one part through a study from start to finish: choose a study, give the part a material, hold it, load it, mesh it, run it and read the result. The Simulation tab is part of the ribbon; the tab's buttons are listed in chapter 2. Studies run on CalculiX; without it a study cannot run (Troubleshooting).

Some of these panels are being reworked, so a field may move or change name in a later update. This chapter describes the panels as they are now.

Before you start

  • Open a part that has a solid body. With no solid, New Study says "The document has no solid body to study."
  • Give the part a material first if you can. A new study starts with the part's material; a part with none uses steel.
  • Units in a panel are set in the panel (a units box beside its value).

1. Choose a study

Click New Study on the Simulation tab. The list is grouped:

GroupStudies
General SimulationStatic, Frequency
Design InsightTopology Study, Design Study
Advanced SimulationThermal, Buckling, Fatigue, Nonlinear, Linear Dynamic
Specialized SimulationDrop Test, Pressure Vessel Design

The new study is named for its type ("Static 1", and so on) and starts with the CalculiX solver, steel and a Gmsh mesh. It gets a tab along the bottom of the window and a study tree below the feature tree: the part with its material, Connections, Fixtures, External Loads, Mesh and Results (a thermal study has Thermal Loads instead of Fixtures and External Loads). Click Model or a Motion Study tab and the study tree folds away; click the study tab to bring it back.

If you are not sure which study you need, open Simulation Advisor: it asks five questions (deformation or stresses, load and unload cycles, sudden collapse, shaking, temperatures) and makes the study for you, then walks the steps in order (Study, Bodies and Material, Interactions, Mesh and Run, Results).

Use Static for this walkthrough: it finds how much the part moves and how highly it is stressed under steady loads.

2. Give the part a material

Right-click the part in the study tree and choose Apply/Edit Material, or click Apply Material. The Material dialog opens with the buttons Apply, Close, Save, Config... and Help along the bottom.

  • Pick a material in the library and click Apply. The part and the study take it, and the status bar says "<study>: <material> applied".
  • The values the study uses are on the Properties tab: Elastic Modulus, Poisson's Ratio, Shear Modulus, Mass Density, Tensile Strength, Compressive Strength, Yield Strength and the thermal rows. Library materials are read only.
  • To use your own values, make a custom material: edit the values, then click Save. The status line says "Material properties: edit the values, then Save." and the material is kept under Custom Materials.
  • The Tables & Curves tab has a curve type (Stress-Strain curve, Temperature-dependent Elastic Modulus, Temperature-dependent Thermal Conductivity, Fatigue SN Curves) and a table of X and Y points, twelve rows, that you fill in for a custom material. Under the table the curve is drawn as a graph: Strain against Stress (N/m^2), Temperature (K) against Elastic Modulus or Thermal Conductivity, or Number of cycles (log) against Alternating Stress for an S-N curve. Points can be typed for a custom material only.
  • A study's material follows the part's: apply a material to the part, including with Apply in this dialog, and the study takes it in one Undo step.
  • A Nonlinear study with Plasticity - von Mises offers Use stress-strain curve when the material has a stress-strain curve; ticked, the study solves from the curve on the Tables & Curves tab.
  • A library material is read only. Its Model Type and failure criterion can still be changed, but the change is taken back and a note tells you to copy the material into Custom Materials to edit it.
  • A new study's Plain Carbon Steel has a yield strength of 2.206e+08 and a tensile strength of 3.998e+08 N/m^2 (the legend says "Yield strength: 2.206e+08").

Apply refuses a material the study cannot use, and says why:

WhenMessage
Any study"<name> has no Elastic Modulus, so the study cannot use it. Pick another material, or give a custom one its Elastic Modulus."
Thermal"<name> has no Thermal Conductivity, so the thermal study cannot use it. ..."
Nonlinear (Plasticity - von Mises)"<name> has no yield strength or elastic modulus: Plasticity - von Mises needs both."
Fatigue"The material has no ultimate strength for its S-N curve: ..."
Thermal stress"The study's material has no thermal expansion coefficient: apply a material that has one."

3. Hold the part (fixtures)

Click Fixtures on the Simulation tab (or right-click Fixtures in the study tree). A fixture says which faces cannot move. Pick the faces in the view; click a face again to take it out. The message box at the top shows one line for the type you chose, for example "The faces do not move or turn."

  • Standard: Fixed Geometry (the default), Roller/Slider and Fixed Hinge.
  • Advanced: Use Reference Geometry and On Flat Faces, with a box for a plane, a flat face or a straight edge to measure from; Symmetry (the cut face holds its normal translation); Cyclic Symmetry (a face on each side and an axis; the sector angle must divide 360); On Cylindrical Faces (Radial, Circumferential and Axial, in the faces' own cylindrical frame); and On Spherical Faces (Radial, Latitude and Longitude; Radial and Latitude go together at 0, and all three at 0 hold the face; other combinations, or a face reaching its pole, say why when you click OK).
  • Translations: choose Units (mm, cm, m, in or ft), then tick Along Dir 1, Along Dir 2 or Normal and give a value for each. The boxes for a translation show only once its tick box is ticked, and the Reference and Translations groups show only for Use Reference Geometry and On Flat Faces.
  • Type and Split tabs: the panel opens on Type. On the Split tab pick a plane, a flat face or a sketch and click Create Split: the part is split along it (a "Split Line" item), the study's mesh uses it and the fixtures and loads move to the matching faces. The mesh is redone at the next run.
  • Symbol Settings: Edit Color... and a symbol size from 20 to 500 (100 at first) set how the fixture symbols look.

Each fixture is named Fixed-1, Roller/Slider-1 and so on. Double-click one in the tree to edit it. A study needs at least one fixture; a Drop Test does not.

The fixture panel's title follows the type, "Standard (Fixed Geometry)" or "Advanced(Use Reference Geometry)". The Standard and Advanced groups open one at a time, and the faces list moves into the open group.

With no study, a fixture says "Make a study first (Simulation > New Study)."

4. Load the part (external loads)

Click External Loads and choose a type.

  • While a load panel is open the picked faces are drawn light blue; for a Force, purple arrows on each face show the way it acts (into the face for Normal, along the picked edge, face or plane for Selected direction) and turn with Reverse direction. One arrow is drawn at the middle of each face; the load is spread over the whole face. The drawing goes when the panel closes.
  • Force/Torque: pick the faces the force acts on (an edge or a vertex is not taken). Choose Normal or Selected direction (then pick an edge, face or plane for the direction), a unit system (SI, English (IPS) or Metric (G), so the value reads in N, lbf or kgf; in an inch part it opens in English (IPS) lbf), a value (100 at first) and Reverse direction. Per item and Total show only when two or more faces are picked, and Per item is the default. Selected direction with no pick says "Pick the direction (an edge, face or plane)."
  • Pressure: pick the faces the pressure acts on. In the Type box choose Normal to selected face (the default) or Use reference geometry, then pick a plane, a flat face, an edge or an axis: the pressure times the area of the faces acts along it, spread over the faces by area. Choose a unit (N/m^2, psi, kgf/cm^2, N/mm^2 (MPa) or ksi; in an inch part it opens in psi) and a value (1 at first), and Reverse direction if needed. Editing a pressure can change its Type in place.
  • Gravity: pick a plane or flat face (gravity acts along its normal) or a straight edge. The unit is m/s^2, in/s^2 or ft/s^2 and the value is 9.81 at first. Reverse direction turns it round.
  • Torque: on the Force/Torque panel choose Torque at the top. Pick the faces and an axis (an axis, a straight edge or a round face; with none, the round faces' own axis), a unit system (SI reads N.m, English (IPS) lbf.in, Metric (G) kgf.cm) and Reverse. The torque is spread as tangential forces that grow with the radius.
  • Bearing Load: pick round faces, a direction across the axis (a plane, face or edge), a force and Reverse, and a Sinusoidal or Parabolic distribution over the half of the face that faces the load. The pushes add up to the force.
  • Centrifugal: an axis (an axis, a straight edge or a round face) and an angular velocity in rad/s, RPM or Hz, applied to the whole part. You can also give an angular acceleration (rad/s^2 or RPM/s), with or without an angular velocity.
  • Remote Loads/Mass: choose the Type, Load/Mass or Displacement, and the Connection, Rigid or Distributed. Pick the faces and a location X Y Z. Rigid ties the point to the faces as one stiff body. Distributed (direct transfer) spreads the load, mass or displacement over the faces without stiffening them. For a load, tick Translational Components (a force) and Rotational Components (a moment); for a displacement, tick each translation or rotation you want to set (unticked is free). A remote mass takes its mass and, for Rigid, the moments of inertia Ixx, Iyy, Izz, Ixy, Iyz and Ixz; an inertia that cannot exist is refused. The rotation and inertia rows are hidden for Distributed. A remote mass weighs under Gravity and spins under Centrifugal. An older Remote Load is converted when you edit it and keeps its name.
  • Distributed Mass: the faces and a mass in kg, g or lb, spread over the faces by area. It weighs under Gravity, spins under Centrifugal and counts in a Frequency study.

The Force panel shows Direction only for Selected direction, and a load panel shows no control that is greyed.

Loads show in the study tree under External Loads; double-click one to edit it.

Connections

Connections (right-click it in the study tree) tells the solver how the parts of an assembly touch.

  • Component Interactions: Bonded, Contact or Free, for all components at once (Global) or per component. The component list and the Friction row are hidden when Global is ticked or the type is not Contact.
  • Contact Sets: Contact (with an optional Friction coefficient), Bonded, or Free. A Free set is one tree item ("Contact Set-N") the solver never sees: its faces are left out of the Global and Component Interactions, so the parts pass through each other there. Suppressing a set gives its faces back to the Global Interaction.
  • Spring, Pin and Bolt open one Connectors panel with a Type list; changing the type reopens the panel on that type, and the faces you picked stay. Bearing and Rigid keep their own panels. Bolt Material is Library (Alloy Steel, Plain Carbon Steel, Stainless Steel, Titanium Alloy, Aluminium Alloy, Brass) or Custom (elastic modulus in N/mm^2 and Poisson's ratio); the other choice's boxes are hidden. The bolt stiffness uses the chosen material. An unknown material, a custom modulus of 0 or less, or a Poisson's ratio outside 0 to 0.5 is refused with a message. The Pin, Bearing and Rigid connectors solve as one stiff body or are held by stiff axial springs, so they run on Windows too.
  • Local Interactions: Interaction is Manually select or Automatically find local interactions (the Find button fills Set 1 and Set 2 with every pair of faces of two bodies that lie face to face, and lists them). Then the Type (Contact, Bonded or Free), Set 1, a swap button and Set 2. Properties: Gap range to consider contact (Contact; 10 percent of the model size at first, shown in mm: faces that far apart are paired) or Gap range for bonding (Bonded; 0.01 percent), and the Coefficient of friction (Contact). Shrink Fit, Virtual Wall, Self-Contact, Stabilize and Contact offset are not offered.
  • Component Interaction (the panel's title is singular): a Message, the Interaction Type (Bonded, Contact or Free), the components with a Global Interaction option, and Properties: for Bonded a gap range for bonding (faces up to that far apart, face to face, are tied), for Contact the Coefficient of friction.
  • Rigid connector: right-click Connections and choose Rigid...; pick a face on each of two parts. The two faces keep their places to each other in all six directions, as if they were one stiff body.
  • Bearing connector: pick a round face on the shaft and a round face on the housing on one axis. Choose Rigid (tied across and along the axis) or Flexible (Lateral and Axial stiffness in N/mm; the boxes show only for Flexible). The shaft always turns freely about the axis; Allow self-alignment also frees the tilt, and Stabilize shaft rotation adds a soft torsion spring so a shaft held only by bearings still solves.
  • Bolt connector, Pre-load: Axial (N) or Torque (N.m, with a Friction factor K, shown only for Torque; the axial force is the torque divided by K times the diameter). The shank stretches and the parts clamp.
  • Bolt, Pin (with retaining ring and key) and Spring connectors work as before.

A fixture on a face of a study with several solids holds only that solid's nodes, so a shaft passing through a fixed hole is not fixed with it.

5. Mesh the part

The mesh cuts the part into the small pieces the solver works on. Click Create Mesh (in an inch part its sizes are in inches); a window titled Mesh Progress reads "Meshing: <part> (1 of 1)" while it works. A new study meshes at Standard, with no curvature refinement (a 60-hole plate meshes with about 151,000 nodes instead of about 600,000), so a part with many small holes takes far less time. Right-click Mesh for Mesh and Run, Apply Mesh Control, Create Mesh Quality Plot (once the part is meshed) and Details.

The Mesh panel has two tabs, Definition and Mesh Quality.

  • Mesh Density: a slider from Coarse to Fine in eleven steps, set to the middle at first. Reset puts it back. Issue warning for distorted elements is ticked at first; unticked, a mesh with distorted elements is kept instead of refused. The middle element size is the part's diagonal divided by 25, and at least 0.5 mm; Coarse doubles it and Fine halves it.
  • Mesh Parameters: the title has a check box; unticked, the size is the slider's Standard size, and ticking the box opens the group. Ticked, choose Curvature-based mesh (the default: Max element size, Min element size, set to a fifth of the max, and the Min number of elements in a circle, 8) or Standard mesh (one Global size). The units are mm or in. For a standard mesh there is also a Tolerance (5 percent of the size at first; it follows the density slider) and Automatic transition, which makes the mesh finer on curved faces under the global size.
  • Mesh Quality tab: one Specify group with a High: list, four buttons that move bodies between the lists and a Draft: list. A Draft mesh is quicker and rougher, for a first look. Bodies split between High and Draft are refused, because FreeCAD meshes at one quality.
  • Advanced: Jacobian points for a high quality mesh (4 points, 16 points which is the default, 29 points or At Nodes; hidden for Draft, where each element has one Jacobian). Advanced shows only Jacobian points.
  • Options: Save settings without meshing keeps the settings and does not mesh; Run is hidden while it is ticked.

A finer mesh gives a more exact answer and takes longer. A good habit: run once on a coarse mesh to see if the loads and fixtures are right, then refine.

After a high quality mesh the curved elements are checked at the Jacobian points; a mesh with distorted elements is refused, with the count, unless Issue warning for distorted elements is unticked.

Mesh Control

Mesh Control (right-click Mesh) makes the mesh finer where you need it. Pick faces, edges or vertices (Selected Entities), then set the Mesh Density slider (Reset puts it back to half the study's global size), the Units, the Element size and a Ratio from 1 to 10: the elements are that size at the picked entities and grow by the ratio to the global size. Adding or editing a control clears the mesh. Double-click a control to edit it.

Mesh Quality Plot

Create Mesh Quality Plot colours the outside of the mesh element by element and draws the colour bar and title block like a result plot. Choose Aspect Ratio (the longest edge over the shortest altitude; 1 is a regular tetrahedron) or Jacobian (distorted elements show red). Clear it like a result plot.

You do not have to mesh before you run: Run makes the mesh itself when it is missing.

6. Run the study

A static study with no loads ends with a message ("Static 1 ran, but nothing moved: the study has no loads. Add one under External Loads...") instead of an all-zero plot. Click Run This Study. A run window shows the stage the run is at, a bar and the elapsed time ("Elapsed Time: 0m:00s"). For a nonlinear or transient study a line under the bar reads the solver's own progress, for example "Step 1, Increment 3, Iteration 2, Solution time: 0.3 of 1 s", and the bar follows the solution time; a linear run does not show that line. Pause and Cancel stop it where it is or end it, and More >> shows the solver's log. The rest of the program stays usable while it runs. When it ends, the first plot (Stress1 for a static study) is shown on the model with its colour legend, and the status bar says "<study> finished." with a summary. If no plot can be shown, a Run This Study box gives the summary instead. If the part has changed since it was meshed, Run meshes it again first and the Report view says "<part> changed since it was meshed: meshing it again." After a linear static run whose largest displacement is more than 2% of the part's size, a question asks whether to solve again with Large displacement on: Yes solves again, No keeps the small-displacement results, Cancel ends with no results. An ordinary run asks nothing. If a run finishes but nothing moved, it says "<study> ran, but nothing moved: the loads reached no part of the mesh. Check that the fixtures and loads are on the part's faces, then run again."

If CalculiX runs out of memory (exit code -9 or 137, or a Windows out-of-memory status) the message says so in plain words. A study that is made the active one takes the previous study's plot off the model. In a document in inches and pounds the run summary is in psi and in. If CalculiX crashes on all cores, the study is run again on one thread and the Report view says "CalculiX crashed (exit code N): running it again on one thread." The study carries on by itself.

7. Read the results

After a run, Results in the study tree lists the plots: Stress1, Displacement1, Strain1. A plot shows on the model with its colour bar, title block and a mark at the largest value.

  • Double-click a plot to show it. Right-click Results for Solver Messages, List Stress, List Displacement and List Strain, Save All Plots as JPEG, Print, Save As and Copy, and Delete; and for Define Factor Of Safety Plot..., Define Stress Plot..., Define Displacement Plot... and Define Strain Plot.... Right-click a plot for Hide or Show, Edit Definition..., Chart Options..., Settings..., Probe, List Selected, Deformed Result (not for Thermal, Fatigue or factor-of-safety plots), Compare Results... and Delete.
  • Stress colours the model from the least to the most stressed places. Compare the largest value with the material's Yield Strength on the Properties tab.
  • Displacement shows how far each point moved; Strain how much the material stretched.
  • Compare Results on the Simulation tab lists every study side by side: its type, whether it ran, the largest stress and displacement, and the lowest frequency or buckling factor. Show makes one study the active one.

Edit a plot

A plot's panel has three tabs, Definition, Chart Options and Settings, right under its title. Deformed shape has a check box in its group title.

  • Definition: the quantity and its component (for Stress, von Mises is first; for Displacement, UX, UY, UZ or URES, with URES at first), and the units (for stress N/m^2, psi, kgf/cm^2, N/mm^2 (MPa) or ksi). Advanced Options has a vector plot for displacement. Deformed shape is Automatic, True scale or User defined, with the scale factor. Property sets the plot's title text and the view it opens in (Front, Back, Left, Right, Top, Bottom or Isometric).
  • Chart Options: show the minimum and maximum marks, the plot details and the legend; use the automatic range or set a maximum and minimum; choose where the legend sits (position buttons, which are pictures, or x and y, 80 and 20 at first) and its size (Thick, Normal or Thin); the number format (Scientific at first, Floating or General) and decimals (3); the colours (Default, Rainbow, Gray Scale or Optimized for Colorblindness), how many (10) and flip; and a marker at the yield strength and a colour for values above a limit. The minimum and maximum marks are off at first; when ticked they show as boxed callouts with a leader to the node. The yield strength line under the legend has a red arrow, and a red mark on the bar when the yield strength is inside the range.
  • Settings: Fringe options (Point, Line, Discrete or Continuous, which is the default); Boundary options (None, Model or Mesh, with a colour); and Superimpose model on deformed shape, with a translucency.

Animate, clip and compare a plot

Right-click a shown plot for Animate..., Section Clipping... and Iso Clipping... (after Settings...).

  • Animate: Play, Pause, Stop and Restart; Frames (5 at first) run from the model as it is to the deformed shape; a Speed slider; Animate in both directions; and Save as AVI file (with a path, a browse button and View with AVI viewer). The AVI is written by the add-on itself: frames are taken from the view with the legend and title.
  • Section Clipping: six section toggles; each is Planar, Cylindrical or Spherical, from the Front, Top or Right Plane, with a Distance (or Radius), Rotation X and Y, and Flip. Show section plane draws the plane while the panel is open (it is a little larger than the model on each side). Also Plot on section only, Show contour on uncut portion of the model, Clipping on and off, and Reset. The cut faces show the plot's values inside the part, in the plot's fringe and boundary settings. Planar, Cylindrical and Spherical are small picture buttons (the name is in the tooltip). OK keeps the clipping on the plot; Cancel removes it.
  • Iso Clipping: six iso toggles, an iso value with a slider over the plot's range, Flip, Plot on iso surface only, Show contour on uncut portion and Reset and Clipping as above.
  • Showing another plot, Hide, or Save All Plots stops the animation and removes the clipping. On a clipped plot the Superimpose ghost is drawn cut like the plot, and Probe reads the interpolated value on the cut faces.
  • Compare Results (the Simulation tab, the Results folder or a shown plot): choose All studies with this mesh (the shown plot's kind in every study that has results) or Define (up to four of every study's plots). OK splits the graphics area into one, two (side by side) or four (2 x 2) views, each with its own legend and a "Study: Plot" name. Turning, panning or zooming one view moves all of them. Exit Compare puts back the single view. Showing any plot ends the comparison.
  • List Result Force (right-click Results): choose Reaction force or Free body force; pick faces on the model, in the tree or on the shown plot; Units are SI (N), English (IPS) (lbf) or Metric (G) (kgf); Update. The panel lists each face's Sum X, Y and Z and its Resultant, and a table for Selection against Entire Model (every fixture face, as a reaction). The force is the integral of stress times the face normal over the face's outer mesh triangles. Faces only; Contact/Friction and connector forces are not offered.

Factor of safety

Define Factor Of Safety Plot... is a three-step panel with back and next arrows at its top right; each step's group says "Step n of 3".

  1. Choose the criterion (Max von Mises Stress, Max Shear Stress (Tresca), Mohr-Coulomb Stress, which is offered only when the material has a Compressive Strength, Max Normal Stress or Automatic) and an upper limit.
  2. Choose the units and the strength to compare with (Yield strength, Ultimate strength or User defined; Automatic uses the yield strength) and a multiplier.
  3. Show the factor of safety across the part, or only the areas below a value you give. The panel also says "Lowest factor of safety: N".

Units, meshes and reports

The report's Study Results has one table per plot (Name, Type, Min, Max), then the plot picture with its caption, and its Mesh information lists the same rows as the Mesh Details window; a load direction never prints "-0". In an inch part new plots read in psi, in and degrees Fahrenheit, and the report uses English (IPS) units for the model sizes, mass, material values, mesh sizes and loads. In a frequency study with no fixtures, List Resonant Frequencies and the report list every mode, and the rigid-body modes read 0 Hz. The Mesh row in the study tree gets a green check once the part is meshed, and Details lists Mesher Used, Max and Min Element Size, Total nodes and the rest down to Time to complete mesh and Computer name, in the part's units. The New Study panel fits without scrolling. When you mesh again after a result exists, a question says that remeshing will delete the results. Red error lines in every panel are readable in Dark, a plot's legend and header draw light on a dark view, and a mode shape plot (Amplitude) scales below 1 on its own. Contact (no penetration) in an assembly gives correct results, node-to-surface, also with a High quality mesh.

Probe and List Selected

Probe reads values off the plot. Choose At location (then click on the shown plot), From selected entities or By node number. A results table and a summary table (value and unit) list what you picked. Report Options are Save, Plot, Capture and Copy (pictures, each with its name in the tooltip), and Annotations puts a mark on the model (node, X, Y, Z, value). List Selected lists the values on the faces, edges or vertices you picked.

More study types are described in chapter 2.

Troubleshooting a study

All of these messages have the title Run This Study.

MessageWhat to do
"There is no study yet: start one with New Study."Make a study first.
"The study has no solver: add one from the Run This Study arrow."Open the arrow beside Run This Study and add the CalculiX solver.
"CalculiX was not found (<path>). Install it, or set its path in Edit > Preferences > FEM > CalculiX."One message, also shown when the path points to a program that is not CalculiX. Install CalculiX or correct the path, then start FreeCAD again.
"The solver stopped because the mesh has distorted elements. ..."Mesh again with a finer density, or fix thin or sharp features in the part.
"The solver stopped: <first error line from CalculiX>" or "The solver stopped (exit code N)."Read the line: it is CalculiX's own wording of what it did not like. Fix that and run again.
"The solver gave no result."Check the Report view (View, Panels, Report view) for lines from CalculiX, then run again.
"The part is not held: add a fixture (Fixtures in the study tree, or the Fixtures Advisor), then run again."Add a Fixture (Hold the part (fixtures)).
"No material is applied to the part: right-click the part in the study tree, Apply/Edit Material..., then run again."Apply a material.
"The material has no Elastic Modulus: edit the material and enter it, then run again." (and the same for Poisson's Ratio)Edit the material and enter the value.
"The mesh has no solid elements: a surface needs a shell thickness. Study a solid body, or give the surface a thickness, then run again."Study a solid body or give the surface a thickness.
"<part> has no geometry to mesh. Fix the part's failed features, then run again."A feature in the part failed; fix it in the feature tree.
"Meshing failed: Gmsh made no mesh of <part>."The part may have slivers or self-intersections. Repair the part, or try a larger or smaller element size.
"CalculiX crashed (exit code N), so the study has no results. Run it again; if it crashes again, make the mesh coarser, or install CalculiX again."Do what it says.

Other things to check:

  • A load is on the wrong side: use Reverse direction in its panel.

For a CalculiX crash and other problems that need another program see Things that need another program.
