3. Sketching

Sketches are where most parts start. The sketch tools snap while you draw and add the relations you would otherwise add by hand, so a sketch keeps its shape when you change a dimension.

Starting and leaving a sketch

  • Start: pick a plane or a flat face, then Sketch (on the pop-up toolbar or the Sketch tab). With nothing picked, Sketch asks you to pick one. A curved face picked on its own does not start a sketch: the command waits for a flat face or a plane.
  • Dimension editing: Esc in the Modify box, or Enter without a change, leaves no undo step behind, and a click on a dimension that does not move it leaves none either. A double-click on a feature's own dimension in the graphics area (for example the extrude depth) opens its Modify box named like D1@Boss-Extrude1, and Enter applies it. Esc in the Modify box while its list of unit suggestions is open closes the box and drops the value (arrow into the list first and Esc only closes the list). Dragging a fully defined (black) line or point does nothing, and leaves no undo step. In a 3D sketch Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y no longer print an error in the Report view.
  • Intersection Curve: picks as follows: a plane gives the one crossing line, a face gives only that face's curve, a plane the sketch lies in, a parallel plane or a vertex give nothing, and an edge picks its body once (two edges of one body give one section). A sketch edge you picked before starting the tool is ignored and the panel just opens. When nothing is made no undo step is left.
  • Sketch tools: Sketch Chamfer with Angle-distance on a rectangle gives 45 degrees on all four corners. Dragging a corner of a rectangle you just drew resizes it instead of moving it whole. Dynamic Mirror copies each Line segment as you place it, and the copies join into one chain. Edit Block then OK on a block you inserted twice keeps both blocks.
  • The sketch origin shows as two red arrows. Points snap to it like any other point.
  • Exit Sketch is drawn pressed while a sketch is open. Offset On Surface leaves the Sketch tab while a 2D sketch is open (it needs a 3D sketch or a surface) and comes back after, and Spline on Surface stays off the flyouts in a 2D sketch.
  • Leave: click Exit Sketch in the confirmation corner (top right of the view), or press Esc twice: the first Esc ends the tool, the second leaves the sketch. The red X leaves without keeping changes. A sketch tool still on when you leave (Smart Dimension, Line, Rectangle and the rest) ends with the sketch: its hint no longer stays on the status bar, with or without a pointer move.
  • Starting a feature from inside a sketch works too: click Extruded Boss/Base and the sketch closes and the extrude opens on it. An empty sketch is deleted when you leave it.
  • Undo: Ctrl+Z works while a sketch tool is running and takes back the last step of that tool. A sketch started by a tool on a picked face or plane is one undo step, so one Undo removes it. After an Undo the old selection is cleared, so the next pick starts fresh. Leaving a sketch you started on a face with nothing drawn leaves no undo step, and Cancel in a sketch tool page that did work for you (such as Linear Sketch Pattern with entities picked first) leaves none either; after OK, the next edit is its own step.

Drawing tools

Line, Centerline, Midpoint Line, Corner and Center Rectangle, 3 Point Rectangle, Parallelogram, Slot, Circle, Perimeter Circle, Centerpoint Arc, Tangent Arc, 3 Point Arc, Ellipse, Partial Ellipse, Parabola, Conic, Polygon, Spline, Point, Text, Construction Geometry, Fillet and Chamfer.

Line tool: click to start and click again for each corner; the line chains on. You can also press, drag and release to draw a single segment. While chaining:

  • Move back to the last end point and away again to switch to an arc. Leave along the line for a tangent arc, sideways for a normal arc. A switches between line and arc.
  • Click on the first point to close the shape.
  • Double-click, or press Esc, to end the chain. Esc again ends the tool.

Snapping

The pointer snaps to what is near it. The yellow square beside the pointer shows the relation the click will add.

Snap targetWhat it adds
End point or centreCoincident
Midpoint of a lineMidpoint
Quadrant of a circlePoint on the circle, level or plumb with its centre
On a line or curveCoincident to that entity
Intersection of two curvesOn both curves
Model edges and vertices (from any shown solid)The same relations, to the model
  • Waking points: hover over a point for a moment to "wake" it. Dotted lines then run from it so you can line up with it.
  • Inference lines: pale dotted lines show when the pointer lines up with a woken point or runs level or plumb from the start point. A line that adds a relation turns blue-violet.
  • Lines that line up: a pale dotted line that runs level or plumb with a woken end point snaps the point exactly there but adds no relation (white square). A line drawn parallel to a woken line takes Parallel, and one at a right angle from a line's end takes Perpendicular. A point on the extension of a line snaps with no relation.
  • Level and plumb lines are tight on purpose: a line only takes Horizontal or Vertical when it is within a pixel or so of level. Very short lines (under about 20 pixels on screen) never take one.
  • Shift while drawing: the length snaps to the grid step (10 mm by default) once the direction is set.
  • Ctrl while clicking: no snap and no relation for that click.
  • F3 turns snapping on and off; the status bar says which.
  • An angle snap in steps of 45° is in Options. It snaps only and adds no relation.
  • Points that stay put: a circle's rim or a 3 Point Arc end placed on a midpoint or a quadrant stays exactly there. Zoomed far out, a line is not pulled level or plumb onto a point that is only near it. A relation that would shrink a line or curve to a point is never added, and an arc with no sweep counts as collapsed. Taking a relation out again puts the geometry back where it was.

Automatic relations

The sketch adds the relation shown in the yellow square when you click: Horizontal, Vertical, Parallel, Perpendicular, Tangent, Coincident, Midpoint and so on. Lines that only line up with something (white square) are placed there but get no relation.

  • Glyphs on the sketch: a Coincident square shows by a point that lands on the origin, a centre or another sketch point, and a Concentric square by two circles or arcs that share a centre. A joint where two curve ends meet has no glyph. They follow View, Hide/Show, Sketch Relations and each relation's own Hide/Show. While you drag a point, a yellow Merge tile shows beside the pointer over a point and an On Edge tile over a curve, and the relation goes on when you let go. The pointer also shows tiles for Merge, Concentric, Equal, Collinear, Symmetric and Fix.
  • Drag to connect: drag a point onto another point, a curve, a midpoint or a model edge, and the relation is added when you let go.
  • Relations that would over-define the sketch are not added.
  • See relations: finished entities carry small green markers. Pick an entity to see its relations in the property panel; pick one there to highlight what it ties together.
  • Add a relation by hand: pick two or more entities (Ctrl-click). The pop-up toolbar and the property panel offer only the relations that fit.
  • Display/Delete Relations lists every relation (Horizontal0 and so on; a corner where two lines simply meet is not a row), with filters for over-defining, external and selected ones. Delete removes the picked row in one undo step. Turning View, Hide/Show, Sketch Relations off hides every relation glyph (dimensions stay), and a relation you add while it is off stays hidden until you turn it on.

Dimensions

  1. Choose Smart Dimension (or the gesture: right-drag up).
  2. Click one or two entities: a line for its length, two lines for the angle between them, a circle for its diameter, an arc for its radius, two points for their distance.
  3. Click where the number should sit. The value box opens.
  4. Type a value (units and sums work) and press Enter, or click the green check. The arrows button flips the direction for horizontal and vertical distances.

The first dimension in a sketch scales the whole sketch so it stays in proportion (switch this off in Options). A dimension that would over-define asks whether to make it a reference (driven) dimension: choose driven and the sketch solves with no colours; choose to leave it driving and the sketch turns over defined, with the yellow overlay and the conflict list, and one Undo clears them. If you untick Driven in the panel for a dimension that would over-define, a note says so and the sketch shows Over Defined.

  • Two circles, or a circle and a point, can be dimensioned horizontal, vertical or aligned, depending on where you place the number.
  • Esc in the value box keeps the dimension at the value it was measured at (it is not removed). Double-click the dimension text later to open the box again; the new value is one undo step.
  • A value box you leave unchanged adds no extra step, so one Undo removes the dimension. When you type a value, the dimension and the value are two steps: the first Undo takes back the value, the second the dimension.
  • A change typed in the dimension's property panel while Smart Dimension is still running is kept.

Sketch tools

Trim (Power Trim by default: drag across the bits to remove), Extend, Offset Entities, Convert Entities (copies model edges into the sketch and keeps them linked), Intersection Curve, Silhouette Entities, Mirror Entities, Move, Copy, Rotate, Scale, Stretch, Linear and Circular Sketch Pattern, Sketch Fillet and Chamfer, Split Entities, Blocks, and Derived Sketch. Each opens a property panel that works as described in chapter 2.

Fully Define Sketch adds the relations and dimensions that leave nothing free. On a large sketch (500 lines) it takes about 17 seconds and ends with 0 degrees of freedom and the status Fully Defined. From 40 entities on, the candidate relations are tried together and those the solver names as redundant or clashing are taken out; relations the sketch already holds (Horizontal, Vertical, coincident points, points on an axis, Equal on the two sides of a rectangle, Collinear on two horizontal or vertical lines) are not tried again; and lines are related to the first line of their direction, so there is one Perpendicular per pair of directions. Smaller sketches try one at a time.

SketchXpert on a large sketch: for a relation given twice, Diagnose takes about 3 seconds; for a clashing dimension it lists every member of the clash and offers to delete each one, newest first (the one you just added). From 60 constraints on, only the constraints on the clashing geometry are tried, and the tries stop after 6 seconds. Small sketches keep the full search.

On a sketch of 125 rectangles where every seventh is tilted, Fully Define takes about 28 seconds (it took over three minutes): a tilted line whose length and direction are known places its other end, and four lines whose opposite sides are parallel are a parallelogram, so their opposite sides share one length.

Pointers and dimension text: the badge on the sketch tool pointers is blue (the Line pointer has a one-line badge, and Smart Dimension's pointer is an arrow with a dimension badge). With units hidden, dimension labels in the view use the document's decimals (64.91), not the full number; a driven dimension keeps its brackets. The Dimension panel has a row of Style buttons and a first precision item "<.12> (Document)"; the dimension text is one box (above, before, after, below), with All uppercase, and Dual Dimension is in its group header. The Modify box shows the full name (D1@Sketch1) and the full value (36.0555 mm; at least the document's decimals), with the units under the value's arrow; Enter on a box you did not touch keeps the exact value.

Quick Snaps: Tangent, Perpendicular and Intersection pick up from anywhere over the curve, and Parallel, Horizontal/Vertical, Horizontal/Vertical to points and Angle alone wake the lines and add their relation.

Convert Entities on an edge of another body that has its own placement (or of a moved body in a part) puts the entities in the right place.

Rotate Entities turns the relations with the shapes. A quarter turn swaps Horizontal and Vertical; another angle drops them, and the lines that were level or plumb become Parallel or Perpendicular to the first of them, so a rotated rectangle keeps its right angles.

Sketch Text and splines after you reopen a file: Edit Sketch Text opens the text and Dissolve Sketch Text works, a Fit Spline keeps following the entities it was made from, and a spline's Curvature Comb, Inflection Points, Minimum Radius and Maintain Internal Continuity settings are still there. The sketch keeps the saved geometry ids instead of tags that change each time the file opens.

Relation glyphs and Make Path: the symbol inside a relation square is drawn solid black (it was a see-through hole), and a Symmetric relation shows one square beside each point instead of arrowheads. Make Path (Tools, Sketch Tools, the sketch right-click Sketch Tools, last in the list) joins the picked sketch entities end to end, open or closed, into one path; entities that do not join are refused with a status-bar line. Picking any entity of a path picks the whole path, and right-clicking one offers Dissolve Path. With something picked and no sketch tool running, Esc clears the picks and the sketch stays open; F5, F6 and the filter keys work inside a sketch.

Quadrant points: the quadrant points of a circle or arc snap while a sketch tool runs (a point at the top or bottom takes On Edge and Vertical to the centre, one at the side On Edge and Horizontal). On a sketch on a model face, snapping to the silhouette of a round face (a cylinder seen edge-on) adds the automatic relation, Coincident to its end or On Edge along it, not just the point.

3D sketches

A 3D sketch draws lines, arcs and splines in space. Press Tab to change the plane the pointer works on. A line's first point keeps the plane you clicked it on, even if you press Tab before the second click, and the ends of arcs share the snapped point as line ends do. If the model under an open sketch loses an edge that the sketch points to, the dangling mark (brown) appears at once. Relations to the model (a point on a vertex, a point on an edge, a spline on a surface, a plane distance) follow the right element when the model changes: redraw a line of the base sketch and widen the pad, and the corner point stays on its own corner.
