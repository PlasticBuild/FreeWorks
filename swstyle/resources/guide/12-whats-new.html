12. What's new in this beta

This page lists the larger changes since the first draft of the guide, by area. Each item points to the chapter that describes it. This is a beta: most of these changes have been tested on the development machine and are now getting their first day-to-day use, so tell us when something does not do what the page says (Reporting a problem).

Sketch

  • Ellipse and Partial Ellipse: clicking an axis end on an existing point puts that point on the new curve. Chapter 3
  • A value that would leave a line with no length or a circle with no radius is refused in the Modify box and the old value is kept. Chapter 4
  • A dimension on a feature that is driven by an equation can be changed in the Modify box. Chapter 4
  • Fully Define Sketch and SketchXpert are much faster on large sketches (500 lines in about 17 seconds); the pointer badges, dimension text and Modify box were reworked. Chapter 3
  • Dragging a fully defined entity does nothing, Esc in the Modify box leaves no undo step, and Intersection Curve picks like the program it is modelled on. Chapter 3
  • Sketch Chamfer gives 45 degrees on all four corners, a just-drawn rectangle resizes from a corner, and Dynamic Mirror copies as you place. Chapter 3

Features, sheet metal and weldments

  • Extrude, Revolve, Sweep and Loft refuse results that cannot be a good solid, and say why. Chapter 4
  • Rib and the other panels with a typed number take what you typed when you click the green check. Chapter 4
  • Insert Part works in a part that has no body yet. Surface-Loft takes 2 to 100 sections. Chapter 4
  • Structural Member has Simple cut and Coped cut, and Allow protrusion. Chapter 4
  • Sheet Metal: if no sheet metal engine loads, each button offers to install the free SheetMetal add-on (chapter 10). Base Flange gained header check boxes. Chapter 4
  • Reference geometry, 3D curves, panel labels and result boxes show the part's own unit, and the decimals you set in Document Properties apply on every panel. Chapter 4, chapter 8
  • Equations read every number in the part's units (an inch part's plain numbers are inches), a dimension can be added to a plain number, and design tables and the Configuration Publisher use the part's units too. Chapter 2
  • Weld Bead opens with a choice ticked in each pair, and the sheet metal Cut list row is drawn in black. Chapter 4
  • Each weld path shows a drawn weld symbol in the 3D view, and the flat pattern's Bounding-Box stays hidden until you show it. Chapter 4
  • Fastening features: Mounting Boss and Snap Hook land where you click; a typed depth or angle that is out of range shows a balloon. Chapter 4
  • Sheet metal: Flatten works after an Edge Flange plus a Closed Hem, the Forming Tool panel keeps the part in view, and Jog measures to the nearest moved face. Chapter 4
  • A configuration can be renamed in the tree (F2 or a second click), deleted with the Delete key, and copied with Ctrl+C and Ctrl+V; its display states follow its name. Chapter 2
  • Surfaces panels have new names and defaults (Knit Surface, Fill Surface); a Base Flange made any way gets the sheet metal folders, and one Undo takes them away together. Chapter 4
  • The Fillet panel builds on surface bodies too, the Surfaces tab's Fillet opens the full Fillet panel for a solid, a Swept Boss takes the guide curve you click after the profile and path, Circular Profile takes the picked sketch as its path, and features under the rollback bar are not rebuilt. Chapter 4
  • Roll Back is several times faster; prompts for a pick are yellow; Shell takes a click on a lofted part. Chapter 4
  • Hole Wizard Tapered Tap with Through All, Vertex chamfer and Filled Surface picks are fixed, and Design Study has a Parameters table. Chapter 4

Assemblies

  • The mate panel has clearer titles and tabs, shows the measured value, and a Concentric or Coincident mate moves the parts the least it can. Chapter 5
  • Suppressing a pattern's seed suppresses its copies; replacing the seed's part changes every copy. Chapter 5
  • Motion Study: a Distance motor has Start Time and Duration, an Oscillating motor has Phase Shift, a small graph shows the motion, and a spring can follow its endpoints. Chapter 5
  • Fasteners: Autosize to mated geometry, a Part Numbers list, Change Fastener Type, Smart Fasteners that find holes made by assembly features, and a Hole Wizard that keeps its Type tab settings. Chapter 5
  • Interference Detection and the other checks skip suppressed components. Chapter 5
  • SpeedPak can remove the ghost of the parts it does not include. Chapter 5
  • Toolbox parts carry a Description (the bill of materials fills it), and the Design Library has a motion folder. Chapter 5
  • Top-down design: the status bar says "Editing Part" while you edit a part in context, and List External References has a Feature's Component column and a List Broken check box. Chapter 5
  • Files save and reopen correctly: suppressed primitives keep their place, in-context sketches keep their assembly, and Esc on "File Not Found" no longer closes FreeCAD. Chapter 5
  • Distance mates between facing flat faces hold them apart by the gap, an in-context sketch follows a moved component, and Toolbox Settings run in a new order with a Customize Hardware grid. Chapter 5
  • Surfaces: Untrim, Planar Surface, Extend Surface, Replace Face and Thicken were fixed. Chapter 4
  • New Part opens its sketch in context, External References... is in the short menu, and the Default configuration has Properties.... Motion Study: Import motion from Basic Motion, motors edited from the tree. Chapter 5
  • An assembly you open from a file drags at once, and What's Wrong names a missing part file in plain words. Chapter 5
  • Rotate Component opens on Free Drag and turns the part like a trackball; the Mate pop-up stays on the screen. Chapter 5
  • Symmetric mates and pattern instances follow a drag live; Explode Line Sketch opens the Route Line panel; Ctrl+Z puts a reordered tree back. Chapter 5

Drawings

  • The ISO sheet formats are redrawn, the drawing tree no longer lists dimension and note rows, and the Bend Table has one row per bend. Chapter 6
  • Drawings of assemblies: hole callouts, the Hole Table and Model Items read the holes made in the assembly. Chapter 6
  • Geometric Tolerance, Surface Finish and Note panels were reworked: symbol order, leaders (including Multi-jog), frame and leader styles, Local and All Around, Insert Datum Feature, and a Document precision for dimensions. Chapter 6
  • Dimension, Geometric Tolerance, Surface Finish, Datum and Note panels follow the new defaults; Bill of Materials Attach to anchor point is off at first; Weld Symbol has its own Properties box; Revision Table has a REVISIONS title row. Chapter 6
  • A new drawing opens the Model View panel and the View Palette; a projected view's panel has an Arrow check box; section labels leave out the scale at the parent's scale; the line weights are 0.25 mm and 0.18 mm; a missing model file is named in a box. Chapter 6
  • A click on the sheet with Note opens the note for typing, and Model Items starts on Entire model. Chapter 6
  • Section hatch uses the ANSI patterns, dimension zeroes follow Document Properties, and Entire model adds shell thickness and sweep diameter. Chapter 6
  • Ctrl+S no longer asks to rebuild every time, the sheet no longer jumps when a panel opens, and the nine drawing panels fit the pane. Chapter 6

View and display

  • RealView: polished metals reflect the studio, the studio can be locked to the model, and the scenes have pictures, four new ones and favourites. Chapter 2
  • Section View keeps the part's colour on the cap and has a header check box for Section by Body. Chapter 2
  • Perspective, shadows, the notification area and the Display Pane button were reworked. Chapter 2
  • Select Midpoint on an edge makes a real midpoint pick. Chapter 2
  • Assemblies: Section View has Section by Component with each component in its own colour, a component can have its own Display Mode, and an image or finish can sit on one face or feature. Chapter 2
  • Reflections, shadows and Ambient Occlusion are faster on big models. Chapter 2
  • RealView chrome shows bright light-panel streaks and stays silver under the Dark theme; assembly Section View has a Transparently Section Components group. Chapter 2
  • Level of detail leaves small components out while you turn an assembly; Appearances and Edit Scene were reworked; the tree rollback bar and the Display Pane headings changed. Chapter 2
  • The floor shadow has a contact layer and a soft layer, Edit Scene has Basic and Advanced tabs (floor size, rotation, environment rotation), and a picked face is blue at any angle. Chapter 2
  • Hidden Lines Removed, Hidden Lines Visible and Wireframe show the background through the faces and draw component edges in assemblies, light edges in Dark, grey parts read clearly in a Dark view, and the Edit Scene lock box is gone (the studio is always locked to the model). Chapter 2
  • Big parts hover and click faster, no outline is drawn while you turn, and a Task Pane slides shut when you drag out of it. Chapter 2
  • Big assemblies are much quicker (RealView, floor reflection, picked-face highlight, outlines); Previous View steps back in order. Chapter 2

3D printing

  • Orca Slicer, Bambu Studio and PrusaSlicer: every setting has its own description, and the lists are built from your own downloaded copy. Chapter 7
  • One 3MF carries object names, colours and plates. Chapter 7
  • A first run in Orca or Bambu shows Set up your printer, and Print3D prepares a part (bed fit, orientation, supports, split to fit) before the slicer. Chapter 7
  • Slicing can be cancelled, shows its progress, and takes imported meshes. Chapter 7
  • Print3D has Settings and Preview tabs, a build plate, printer profiles (Options, Export, Print3D Printers), colours and names for each body in the 3MF, and a clear message when the slicer is not downloaded. Chapter 7
  • The 3D Slicer tab never starts a slicer installed on the computer, and the slicer download shows its size, MB progress and a Cancel button. Chapter 7

Simulation

  • A walkthrough chapter takes a study from material to results. Chapter 11
  • Material curves are drawn as graphs, and the default steel has its yield strength.
  • Fixtures: Symmetry, Cyclic Symmetry, On Cylindrical Faces, On Spherical Faces, Split and symbol settings. External loads: Torque, Bearing Load, Centrifugal (with angular acceleration), Remote Loads/Mass (load, mass or displacement, rigid or distributed) and Distributed Mass. Connections: Free contact sets, a Rigid connector, a Bearing connector and bolt Pre-load. Chapter 11
  • A Mesh panel with Tolerance and Jacobian points, Mesh Control, and a Mesh Quality Plot; Run meshes again when the part changed and warns when nothing moved. Chapter 11
  • Mesh panel with Definition and Mesh Quality tabs, Automatic transition, a warning you can switch off for distorted elements, unit systems for Force and Torque, and a Type for Pressure and Fixture titles that follow it. Chapter 11
  • Result plots can be defined and edited (Stress, Displacement, Strain, Factor Of Safety), with Probe and List Selected. Chapter 11
  • Random Vibration and Response Spectrum studies are offered. If CalculiX itself crashes, the study is run again on one thread. Chapter 2, chapter 10
  • Run This Study has a run window with Pause and Cancel; plots can be animated, clipped (section and iso) and compared in up to four synced views; List Result Force; Connectors and Local Interactions panels; Nonlinear studies can use the material's stress-strain curve. Chapter 11
  • The run window shows the solver's step, increment and iteration, the plot panels' tabs sit under the title, and a large displacement asks whether to solve again with Large displacement on. Chapter 11
  • Inch parts open the Force, Pressure and Mesh panels in inch units, the Mesh Details list and the green check on the Mesh row are new, and remeshing asks before it deletes results. Chapter 11
  • Contact (no penetration) in an assembly gives correct results; mode shape plots scale on their own. Chapter 11

  • A page for people who already know another CAD program, and step-by-step tutorials. Chapter 13, chapter 14
  • A page of error messages and what to do about each. Chapter 15

  • Ctrl+Tab works over the 3D view and in a sketch, and Ctrl+Shift+Q in a sketch. Chapter 9

  • ScanRuler files open as point clouds, sketches and projects with their measurements; the tab has tooltips and a right-click menu on results. Chapter 2
  • Toolbox sizes reach the Hole Wizard, and the Design Library has a toolbar and search. Chapter 5

  • Panels and boxes hide a control that cannot be used instead of greying it. Chapter 2

  • The Open box's Display State list follows the chosen configuration, and Open read-only lasts for the session only. Chapter 2
