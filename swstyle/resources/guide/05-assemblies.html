5. Assemblies

An assembly places parts and smaller assemblies (components) and holds them together with mates. Assemblies are built on FreeCAD's own Assembly workbench, so assemblies are ordinary FreeCAD files.

Starting an assembly

  1. Ctrl+N, Assembly. The Begin Assembly panel opens.
  2. Pick a part from the list of open documents, or Browse for one. The first part sits on the assembly origin and is fixed: it shows (f) in the tree.
  3. Insert Component adds more. Click in the view to place each one; these float freely and show (-) until mates hold them.

Options in the panel: start this panel for each new assembly, show a preview, insert as a virtual component (kept inside the assembly file), insert as an envelope, and show the rotate toolbar while placing. If a part has configurations, a list lets you choose one.

From a part: File, Make Assembly from Part starts an assembly with the open part already placed and fixed. It asks where to save only when you first save.

Mates

Choose Mate on the Assembly tab, or Ctrl-pick two faces, edges or points with no command running.

  • After the second pick, a small toolbar offers the mates that fit, with the most likely first. The chevron opens the advanced and mechanical ones.
  • Aligned / Anti-aligned flips which way the parts face. Click the button or press Alt.
  • Click a picked item again to take it out.
  • The mate panel stays open (pinned) for the next pair. Undo removes the last batch.
  • Suppressing the seed of a Linear or Circular Component Pattern also suppresses its instances (Set to Resolved brings back only those it took), and Replace Components on a seed changes every instance to the new part.
  • Mirror Components and the Linear pattern take their plane and direction in their own pick boxes; Mate Analysis and the tab arrows show no greyed controls; Explode Line Sketch is on the flyout; a hidden component shows a hollow icon in the tree.
  • Right-clicking a component in the Mate panel no longer shows an empty Reference Geometry Display menu.
  • Mates go into the Mates folder in the tree, named Coincident1, Concentric2 and so on. The mate panel's title reads the same way (Coincident1) and the mate type tabs have a border.
  • A Distance between two flat faces turned toward each other holds them apart by the gap between the two outsides, whichever side the part was on when you made the mate; Flip dimension puts them on the other side, and it takes a click beside its text as well as on its box. Faces that face the same way keep the side they are on.
  • The Distance and Angle boxes show the value measured between the picks, and the pick names read Face<1>@Bracket<1>.
  • A Concentric or Coincident mate made on parts that are nearly there moves them the least it can.
  • A label with its control greyed out is hidden along with it (for example the tabs a mate does not use).

Gear, Rack Pinion, Screw and Belt couple parts while you drag them. A part held by a single Concentric mate drags along its axis and turns round it. A flexible sub-assembly with a Limit Distance or Angle mate moves in the top assembly as it should.

SmartMates: drag a part by a face, edge, vertex or round face onto another and the mate that fits is made: flat faces (Coincident, facing each other), straight edges, vertices, round faces, a cone in a hole, a peg in a hole (Concentric and Coincident; Flip puts the peg through), a flange's pattern of holes (three mates), and a mate reference dropped over a plain hole; Tab during the drag flips the alignment. Coincident of two straight edges leaves the parts free to slide along the line. Belt/Chain: Flip belt side sends the picked member's belt round its inside (the row says "(inside)" and the length follows), Engage belt turns an inside pulley the other way, and typing a Belt length under Driving slides the last pulley that is not fixed until the belt is that long (a length the pulleys cannot make is refused and the panel stays open). Mate Controller: its Add, Update and Delete Position buttons, slider and Calculate/Save Animation show once there is something for them. Replace Mate Entities shows Flip Mate Alignment only for mates that have one.

Moving parts: drag a floating part to move it; mates move only what they must. An assembly you open from a file drags at once, with no double-click first, and a drag in a large assembly is smoother. Rotate Component opens on Free Drag, where a drag turns the part about its centre like a trackball, solving its mates at each step (a part mated flat turns on that face). A Symmetric mate follows a real drag of its first part live, in the drag's own undo step, and instances of a component pattern follow a dragged seed live (a dragged instance stays put in its pattern). Ctrl+Z after a reorder in the tree puts the tree back in its old order. A second pick on the same component in a mate says "Mate selections must be on two different components". The Flip button on the Mate pop-up opens in the right state. The Mate and SmartMates pop-ups stay whole on the screen, and flip to the other side of the pointer when there is no room. Move Component and Rotate Component offer collision detection. Move Component and Rotate Component show Check between (These components or All components) under both Collision Detection and Physical Dynamics, and To XYZ Position puts the picked point (or the origin) at the numbers you type. In the top assembly, right-click a part that sits inside a subassembly: Fix / Float and Delete act on the subassembly itself (one Undo step there) and the top copy follows. Assembly feature panels and Replace Components show no greyed controls. Conflicting mates show a red mark; What's Wrong explains them (a part whose file is missing reads: The file "name.FCStd" for component name<1> could not be found), and Auto Repair re-attaches mates whose faces have changed.

Patterns and copies

Linear, Circular, Pattern Driven (follows a feature pattern in a part), Sketch Driven, Curve Driven and Chain patterns, plus Mirror Components. In these panels a box that belongs to an option stays live and switches that option on when you use it: typing a spacing turns Equal spacing off, typing a chain count turns Fill path off, clicking the reference point box picks Selected point, clicking the face box turns on Face normal, changing Direction 2 of a Circular Pattern turns Symmetric off, and typing a mirror prefix, suffix or folder ticks its check. Controls that cannot apply are not shown: Mirror's Back on step 1 and Next on the last step, Chain Spacing for a linkage, Smart Fasteners' Properties box until a fastener group is picked, and a nut's Length row. Copy with Mates copies a component and re-picks its mates. Ctrl-drag a component in the view to copy it, or drag a part from the tree into the view to add another.

Assembly features

Cuts, holes and fillets made in the assembly (they exist only there), weld beads, and assembly-level patterns. Edit Component opens a part in place so you can change it using the other parts as references. Convert Entities used there keeps the top-down relation in the tree, a Mate Reference on a feature of a body that has its own placement gives mates, and Lock Rotation keeps the turn you gave a mate.

Exploded views

Exploded View opens a panel with the explode steps and settings. Pick components, drag the arrow to move them, or type a distance and angle. "Auto-space components after drag" spreads several parts evenly. The reverse buttons in the panel are pictures, not text arrows. Explode lines (smart or sketched) show how parts go together; in an exploded view the CommandManager's Explode Line Sketch opens the Route Line panel (with Jog Line and Smart Explode Lines), not a plain 3D sketch. An exploded view stays exploded when you rebuild (Ctrl+B): the components go back to their mated positions before the recompute and the steps apply again afterwards. A file saved exploded reopens exploded, and mates are not judged while the view is exploded; Collapse brings them back. A radial step has Explode from bounding box center (off: the parts move out from the axis; on: straight out from their common centre), and its Auto-space controls are hidden because they only space regular steps. The Route Line (Explode Line Sketch) panel shows Undo Last Line, Jog Line, Jog distance, Flip Jog and Undo Last Jog once they can be used. Each exploded view is kept with its configuration; right-click it to Animate Explode or Collapse. Deleting a configuration deletes its exploded views with it.

Motion studies

The Motion Study tab at the bottom of the view opens the timeline under the view. On a part, the turns, seconds and axis you set are used each time you press Play and when you save the animation; changing one stops and restarts from time 0.

  • Study types: Animation, Basic Motion and Motion Analysis.
  • Toolbar: Calculate, Play from Start, Play, Stop, playback speed, Save Animation, Animation Wizard, Autokey, Motor, Spring, Damper, Force, Contact, Gravity, Results, Properties.
  • Drag the time bar and move parts to set keys; diamonds mark each change.
  • Motors can be rotary or linear: constant speed, distance, oscillating, segments, data points or an expression. A Distance motor has Displacement, Start Time and Duration: it stays put before the start, moves over the duration and then holds (a motor made before these boxes existed keeps the whole study). Oscillating adds Phase Shift. A small graph under the Motion box plots the motion over the study, including the Segments, Data Points and Expression functions.
  • Spring: Update to model changes makes the Free length follow the distance between the endpoints, read when the study is calculated; while it is on, the length box is hidden and a line shows the length it takes. With Damper on, Exponent of damper force expression and Damping constant appear, and the exponent is used in the force.
  • Animation Wizard, Import motion from Basic Motion / Import motion from Motion Analysis: shown in an Animation study once that study has been calculated as Basic Motion (or Motion Analysis). The calculated frames become key points over a Duration from a Start Time (the default is the calculated duration).
  • Motors, springs and the rest are edited from their rows in the Motion Study tree (double-click; right-click Edit Feature, Delete, Check Interference...; or the Delete key); there is no motor list beside the timeline. The tree's top row is the assembly with its configuration and display state. The filter and zoom buttons are small icon buttons with tooltips.
  • The Mate panel's Distance and Angle boxes stay live and show the picks' measure; typing a value or using the arrows chooses that mate. On the Linear, Circular and Curve Driven Component Pattern panels, Reverse Direction is the two-arrow button left of the direction box. Tools, Defeature... opens the assembly Defeature panel.
  • The toolbar shows what the study type uses: Gravity, Spring and Contact are hidden in Animation, and Damper, Force, Results and Plots appear only in Motion Analysis. The Spring panel shows the damper value with Damper, and the exponent and Display only for linear springs. The Contact panel shows materials with Material and coefficients with Friction or Static friction. In the Animation Wizard, Back, Next and Finish show only on the pages they work on, and Explode and Collapse only with an exploded view. The timeline key menu and Key Properties at time 0 offer only what applies.
  • While a part is edited in the assembly the status bar says "Editing Part", and "Editing Assembly" again after you exit. List External References has a Feature's Component column (the instance the reference was made in), an "Assembly: <file>" line and a List Broken check box (shown only when there are broken references; off leaves them out). A sketch that uses four edges of one reference is one row, not four.
  • New Part: its sketch opens in the assembly's context, so the plate stays drawn and can be picked, and the new part lands on the face you clicked. While a sketch is open in context a click on another component's face, edge or vertex goes into the selection (Ctrl adds; a second click removes it), so Convert Entities can use it. The status bar shows one definition state, and Edit Component shows pressed while a part is edited. External References... is in the short right-click menu of a part that has in-context references. Save Part (in External File): OK keeps a name still being typed, and says so if the file is open in FreeCAD, already exists or its folder is missing. The Default configuration of a new part or assembly has Properties... on its right-click menu: the same Configuration Properties page, and a new name typed there renames Default.
  • An in-context sketch follows its component when you move it and rebuild, and Unlock All catches the references up.
  • Edit In Context: on a part feature made in context (right-click the feature or sketch, below List External Refs), this opens the assembly the references came from and edits the part there. Move with Triad shows only the triad (the tree stays and no letters are drawn) and a click in empty space ends it. Edit Component shows the edited part blue ("Use specified colors when editing parts in assemblies", on at first). The mate pop-up has Lock rotation for Concentric mates, matching the panel.

Motion Analysis is basic: good for checking how a mechanism moves, not for engineering loads.

Configuration specific properties: in Component Properties, Referenced configuration, Exclude from bill of materials and Solve as (Rigid or Flexible) follow Change properties in: This configuration, All configurations or Specify configuration(s). They are kept in the assembly's configuration table (the CompCfg_, Excl_ and Flex_ columns), Show Configuration applies them and they survive save and reopen. With no configuration table the component just changes.

Saving and reopening

These cases save and reopen correctly:

  • Suppressed features: a suppressed box, cylinder or other primitive remembers its place in the file, so Unsuppress, a configuration or Undo puts it back where it was and not at the origin.
  • A part changed while the assembly was closed: open the assembly again after you saved a part with a different size (a Pad changed from 10 to 15, say) and the mates read their faces again on open, so the component follows the part as it is now. If nothing changed the file opens unmodified, with no Save prompt.
  • Editing in context: a sketch on another component's face keeps its reference to that part and the assembly it came from, so Edit In Context still finds the assembly after you save and reopen, and a face or edge reference stays put when the other part is moved or tilted. Save As on the assembly makes the parts' references name the new file. If a part changes under an in-context sketch, the sketch is put back on its face after the rebuild.
  • Renaming a component in the tree (when that option is on): the assembly opens the renamed file, and its components do not need a rebuild.
  • File Not Found: the box can be closed with Esc or its close button.

Large assemblies

Large Assembly Settings, lightweight components, simplified graphics stand-ins per configuration, defeature (a simple stand-in to hand on), envelope publishing, and Large Design Review (opens fast from saved graphics). Large Design Review opens an assembly from its saved graphics in a window that lists the components, subassemblies, History, Sensors and Annotations in the tree (a hidden component is greyed) and shows the assembly tabs and the assembly status. The status bar of an assembly reads "Under Defined Editing Assembly". Opening and dragging assemblies with hundreds of parts is fast because only the parts that can move are solved, and the drawn shapes of parts are kept between uses. In SpeedPak, Options has Remove ghost: with it on, only the faces and bodies you included are drawn, and the rest of the part (normally shown as graphics you cannot pick) is left out. Update SpeedPak and saving keep it. The Options box shows once something is included.

Checking and output

Interference Detection, Clearance Verification, Hole Alignment, Assembly Visualization (sort components by mass or any property), the Bill of Materials, and Pack and Go (copies an assembly with all its parts to a new folder; its nested and flat views and tick boxes are described in chapter 2, Opening, saving and exporting).

Suppressed components are never checked by Interference Detection, Clearance Verification, Hole Alignment or Assembly Visualization, including one suppressed inside a subassembly. Hidden components are left out of Interference Detection while Ignore hidden bodies/components is on (it is at first) and checked when it is off; the other three include hidden components.

The Evaluate panels show only what applies. In Interference Detection, Ignore and Save Results appear when there are results to act on; the Excluded Components list, Remember and Hide stay live, and using any of them turns Excluded Components on. Excluded Components has a check box in its group title as well, which turns it on and off. In Clearance Verification and Hole Alignment, Ignore appears when a result is picked; in MateXpert, Suppress appears when a mate is picked; in AssemblyXpert, Fix appears only on checks that have a fix. Analyze with nothing picked analyzes every component. In Move Component, the collision list and Resume, and Dynamic Clearance's list, Resume, Stop and value, stay live and switch their option on when used.

Isolate, then Save as Display State: Display State-1 keeps the look from before Isolate and the isolated look is Display State-2.

Show Hidden Components: Ctrl+Z after Exit Show-Hidden hides again only the components you picked to show; the rest of the assembly stays as it was. Saving while the mode is on writes the real visibility (shown components are saved shown), and the mode carries on after the save. The File Not Found box that appears when an assembly opens with a missing part must be answered before you go on, as in the program this look is modelled on.

Fasteners

The fastener library inserts standard bolts, screws, nuts and washers, and Smart Fasteners adds the right fastener to each hole. Hole Series makes matching holes through several parts.

Smart Fasteners: Insert, Smart Fasteners first shows a box (the calculation may take time; Don't show again, OK, Cancel). A hole made by an assembly feature (Hole Wizard, Simple Hole or Hole Series) counts as a hole for it: Smart Fasteners puts in the screw the Hole Wizard type names (its size, or the hole's diameter), mates it to the hole, and Autosize reads it. A Hole Series whose last part is tapped ends the stack with no nut, and an assembly feature no longer cuts a fastener that sits in its hole. With no holes it says "No holes were found that support use of Smart Fasteners." and closes.

Hole Wizard in an assembly keeps what its Type tab sets. The options Near side countersink, Under head countersink (the counterbore floor) and Far side countersink (where the hole comes out of the last part) are used. Up To Next (stops where the hole comes out of its first part), Up To Vertex, Up To Surface, Offset From Surface and Reverse offset are worked out from the parts: click the vertex or face on a component while the Type tab is showing; with nothing picked the panel says so and makes nothing. A hole on a round face goes square to it where you click, each hole its own way; a Hole Series keeps flat faces and one direction.

Configure a fastener

Pick a fastener and open Configure Component. The panel's groups are Replace Component, Part Numbers, Properties and Options.

  • Replace Component: Change Fastener Type... lists the standard's Toolbox folders and types. The component changes in place to the nearest size and length of the new type, as one Undo step.
  • Description: a new Toolbox part carries the Description saved in Toolbox Settings, or else the name of its type, and the bill of materials shows it in DESCRIPTION (it was empty). Customize Hardware shows that default, and a description you type wins. Parts already in the Toolbox folder keep the Description they were made with.
  • Part Numbers: the fastener's number, or "Part Number Unassigned". Add is there when none is assigned; Edit and Delete when one is, and Delete returns to the part's own name.
  • Properties: Size, Length and the rest, plus Thread Display (Simplified, read only), a Comment saved with the part and a Configuration Name that follows the size. A nut shows no length or thread length.
  • Options: Autosize to mated geometry appears only when the fastener is mated to a hole of a part. Ticked, the size is the hole's (the Hole Wizard size, else its diameter, to the nearest size the type has) and the length comes from the stack in the hole (the grip, the washer under the head, and the nut and washer at the far end), rounded up to the next standard length and no longer than the standard's longest. It starts ticked when the fastener already follows the hole, turns off when you change the size or length by hand, and applies at OK. Smart Fasteners use the same rule. A fastener mated to a feature hole of an assembly (not a part's round face) is not found.

Toolbox Settings run in this order: 1 Hole Wizard, 2 Customize Hardware, 3 User Settings, 4 Permissions, 5 Smart Fasteners. Customize Hardware shows a grid of the item's configurations (Size with its tick, Finish, Thread Display, Part Name, Specification, Standard, Configuration Name, Designation, Part Number, Description); Part Number and Description can be edited and are kept, and a Custom Properties... button keeps names. A new Toolbox part file starts with its saved part number, description and custom properties.

The Design Library toolbar has Back, Forward, Add to Library, Add File Location, Create New Folder, Refresh, Up one level and Toolbox (Back, Forward and Up show only when there is somewhere to go). The search box ("Search in Design Library" or "Search in Toolbox") filters the list from the folder shown downwards. The Design Library has a motion folder beside annotations, assemblies, features, forming tools, parts, routing and smart components (it is added to a library made earlier too). Right-click a Toolbox item for Configure... (Toolbox Settings), Create Part... (a size and length dialog; a new part document, with the library file untouched), Add to Favorites and Large Icons, Small Icons or List, which is remembered.

Routing

The Routing tab routes pipes between components. A routed pipe never doubles back on itself. Route Properties groups Route Type, Connection Points, Route Specification (diameter, wall), Bends and Options; the Auto Route group appears once both ends are picked. A Connection Point's Type list offers Pipe, Tube, Electrical, User Defined and Clip.
